What a data center project could provide, and where each item goes

The public record rates each item a large data center project could provide in Florida. This page gives each one in the record's own words: where it would go, its verdict and what it depends on. It does not weigh the benefits against costs.

This page is not a county document. County staff should not use it to point community groups to a developer.

Where each item goes

Four homes, each with its own rules

Standards

Every project must meet the county's standards.

County item

The county can charge an impact fee, or use a development agreement, to cover what is needed to handle the impacts its own impact study measures, such as backup power at county facilities.

County land lease

On land the county owns, the county also sets the rent: cash rent at full market value, with rent paid in kind possible on top.

Private agreement

Other benefits, such as computing time or training seats, can come only from a private agreement between the developer and a community group. The county is not a party to that agreement.

Standards: what every project must meet

A county does not need any agreement to protect residents.

The framework's standards ordinance applies to every large data center, whether or not the developer signs anything, and it asks for no benefits and charges only fees that cover the county's own costs of review and inspection:

  • Distance and screening
  • Water
  • Noise
  • Flood and sinkholes
  • Burials and archaeology
  • Fire and hazards
  • End of life
  • Generators, utilities and complaints
  • Public reporting

Every number in that ordinance is set by each county from its own studies.

Evidence: Drafted. The model ordinance has not been reviewed by a lawyer or adopted anywhere.

Drafted in the model instruments (4)

County agreement, Module M2

Emergency power for county facilities

Fits provisionallyCounty itemBuilt or given once

Only as mitigation the county's impact study supports

Mitigation, sized by the county's impact study. Nothing is delivered from the project site.

What it would do

If the grid goes down for days, the emergency operations center and the other county emergency facilities the county's study names could keep their lights, cooling and radios running. At a shelter, the equipment is sized only for people evacuated because of an incident at the data center, not for hurricane sheltering. The equipment sits at the emergency facility, behind that facility's meter. The utility owns it, or the developer leases it to the county, or it is given to the county with money set aside to maintain it.

Private agreement (CBA Clause Bank §8.4)

Dark fiber to schools, colleges and clinics

WorksPrivate agreementBuilt or given once

Private agreement between the developer and a community group; the county is not a party.

What it would do

If your school, college or clinic is near the route, the developer may offer it fiber strands directly, under a private agreement. County-run buildings, including county libraries, cannot receive them this way. This does not bring internet service to your home.

Set aside: in the model instruments, not relied on (4)

These are in the model instruments but are not relied on as benefits. Each is a different kind of promise the test does not cover: demand flexibility is mitigation of grid impact, dig-once conduit is a county purchase, colocation is a service the recipient pays for, and the retired-equipment right is a right to buy at fair value. They stay listed so the public can see why.

Private agreement or county land lease (Module M3, para. T-17)

Preferred colocation and cabinet reservation

Not this kind of benefitPrivate agreementCounty land lease

A priced service the recipient pays for

Private agreement between the developer and a community group; the county is not a party. On county-owned land, it can also be a lease term.

What it would do

The host institution can house, at a discounted price, servers it receives or buys for public use under the agreement, with better power, cooling and security than a server closet. A sales tax rule limits this: until the institution's own load at the site would reach 1 megawatt, its hardware stays elsewhere unless the state Department of Revenue advises otherwise (Module M3, para. T-17(e)).

Utility purchase under its own rules

Larger water or reclaimed water main in the developer's trench

Not this kind of benefit

A utility purchase, not a benefit

The utility pays for the added size. Not a benefit, a condition of approval or mitigation.

What it would do

The utility that serves the site can pay the added cost of a larger pipe while the developer's trench is open, so the same pipe can later serve neighbors.

County purchase under its own rules

County backups at the developer's site in another region

Not this kind of benefit

A county purchase, not a benefit

The county pays for the space like any other customer. Not a benefit, a condition of approval or mitigation.

What it would do

If the developer also runs a data center far from the county, the county can buy space there for a copy of its records and systems, so one storm or attack is less likely to take out both.

County lease under its own rules

County public-safety radio site on the developer's roof or land

Not this kind of benefit

A county lease, not a benefit

The county pays rent like any other tenant. Not a benefit, a condition of approval or mitigation.

What it would do

If radio coverage is weak near the site, the county can rent space on the developer's roof or land for its radio equipment, as it would from any other owner.

The law

What Florida law does not let counties require from a data center

12 of these apply to data centers.

Some items on this page cannot be required by a Florida county because a state law has taken the subject away from counties. A private agreement between the developer and a community group can carry some of them, such as protections for the developer's own workers, because these laws limit counties, not private parties. The county is not part of any such agreement.

State law does not allow these (11)

A Florida law takes this subject away from counties.

  • A share of power for public buildings

    What it would do: Lower power bills at schools and public buildings, so public budgets go further.

  • Wage floor written into a development agreement

    What it would do: A guaranteed pay floor if you work at or for the facility.

  • Health insurance for the facility's workers written into a development agreement

    What it would do: Health coverage if you work at or for the facility.

  • Severance for workers whose jobs are automated, written into a development agreement

    What it would do: Severance pay if your job at the facility is automated away.

  • Paid retraining and first right to new roles for workers whose jobs are automated, written into a development agreement

    What it would do: Paid retraining and first claim on new jobs at the site if automation ends yours.

  • Paid sick and family leave for the facility's workers, written into a development agreement

    What it would do: Paid days off when you or your family are sick.

  • Retirement contributions for the facility's workers, written into a development agreement

    What it would do: Employer contributions toward your retirement.

  • Hiring from hard-hit neighborhoods on public construction

    What it would do: A real path into a construction apprenticeship if you live in a hard-hit neighborhood.

  • Below-market space for child care, nonprofits and makers

    What it would do: Affordable child care or program space close to home.

  • Benefits in a signed agreement protected from later exaction suits

    What it would do: Terms in a signed agreement could not later be undone by a lawsuit.

  • Saving an older coastal building from demolition

    What it would do: The county could review, and possibly stop, the demolition of an older beach-area building you know.

State law may not allow these (1)

A Florida law may take this subject away from counties. The question is open and no court has decided it, but on balance the law leans that way.

  • Banning groundwater for cooling where a supplier pumps it or the data center is under 50 megawatts

What this is not

Passing the test only means an item is not ruled out. It does not mean the item is lawful in a given county, wanted by whoever would receive it, agreed to by a developer, or worth a set amount. Each county item still needs its own written link to the impacts in the county's study, a value, and review by the county attorney. A private agreement item needs the community group's own counsel; the county does not review it.

No benefits under these model instruments have been delivered in Florida.
