Model Instruments for Data Center Development in Florida Counties

Guide, Open Legal Questions and Methodology

Release 2026.4 · In-Kind Standard · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24021153

Companion record (the model instruments, CC0 1.0): 10.5281/zenodo.24021154

Earlier releases of this record: Release 2026.3, 10.5281/zenodo.23482770; and, published as the Automation Resilience and Community Benefit Framework, Release 2026.2, 10.5281/zenodo.22969877, and Release 2026.1, 10.5281/zenodo.22216263.

What this is

A plain-language guide to the model instruments, the legal questions they raise with the answers the primary sources support, and the two tests used to decide what belongs in a county instrument. In-Kind Standard is the name of the citizen-prepared project that writes them. The instruments themselves are in the companion record under CC0, so that a county can adopt them without attribution.

Known limitations

  • No county has commissioned the impact study or the impact fee study. Every mitigation amount depends on one.
  • The author's comparison with adopted ordinances and executed agreements, as of October 2026, found no Florida county that has adopted data center performance standards, no executed ground lease of public land for a data center, no data center community benefits agreement of 2025 or 2026 without a government party, and no executed United States data center agreement that dedicates computing capacity to a community, so those features are untested.
  • Not adopted anywhere. Not reviewed by licensed counsel. The work of one person.
