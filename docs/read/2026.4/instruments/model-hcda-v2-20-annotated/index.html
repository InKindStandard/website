FLORIDA MODEL INSTRUMENTS FOR DATA CENTER DEVELOPMENT

Model Host Community Development Agreement

Model form. Remove this heading block, and every drafting note, before the County first makes the draft public.

What this is. A development agreement under ss. 163.3220 to 163.3243, Florida Statutes, recorded as a declaration of restrictive covenants. It fixes the Project’s capacity and phases, makes the County’s standards (Exhibit “O”) and the Schedule of Supplemental Operating Covenants (Exhibit “S”) covenants running with the land, and, besides the concurrency terms of Section 2.4 and the County’s purchase of spare conduit at cost under Section 4.5, carries only mitigation that Exhibit B-1 measures. It is optional: a project may be approved under the standards without it (Section 10.2), and the County may not require it or consider an applicant’s willingness to enter into it (Section 10.3).

What it does. Article 4 carries a Physical Infrastructure Mitigation Payment and a payment for first responders’ equipment and training, both paid into a restricted Mitigation Account outside the general fund, and, where elected, emergency power for County facilities. It also sets utility, reporting, decommissioning and vesting terms, keeps the covenants for the life of the use (Section 9.4), and provides remedies, including a written denial of further approvals during an uncured mitigation default (Section 8.2(b)).

Works with. Exhibit “O” holds the County’s data center standards as in effect at execution; where both apply, the Standards govern the approval decision and this Agreement its obligations (Section 10.3(b)). Exhibit B-1 holds the findings of a study whose preparer the County selects (Section 4.1(d)). The County’s impact fee ordinance, where adopted, governs each category it covers (Section 4.7(b)), and Section 3.3 uses the phase evidence Module M1 describes. Exhibit “S” and Module M2 also serve a County ground lease. The Drafter’s Notes list each blank and choice.

Before use. First confirm which retail electric provider serves the parcels (Section 5.6), and commission Exhibit B-1 before entering any amount or capability; where it supports none, none is due (Section 4.1(c)). The main elections are Recital D, the Campus Development Envelope (Section 3.2(b)), payment timing (Section 4.2(c)), Module M2 (Section 4.4), the purchase option (Section 7.4(d)) and the Parts of Exhibit “S.” Each reference to Part S-25 of Exhibit “S,” such as in Sections 5.3, 5.4 and 5.6(c), takes effect only where Part S-25 is attached, which Exhibit B-1 must support. Where a bracket names an overlay district, name the district or approval the Project is approved under. Complete Exhibits “A” and “J” before notice of the hearings; with this block, remove the lines above it and the author block, leaving the caption alone for recording (Section 10.22).

Known gaps. No county has adopted this model, and no licensed attorney has reviewed it. No Exhibit B-1 study has been done. Some provisions rest on law that is not settled: the reach of the post-hurricane limits on land development regulations (Section 2.1); administrative review of a later Phase within the Campus Development Envelope (Section 3.2(c)); the status of the training reimbursement and Mitigation Account payments under impact fee and tax law (Sections 4.3(c) and 4.6); withholding approvals and liquidated amounts (Section 8.2(b) and (c)); whether later standards and fees reach the Project (Section 10.8); security draws after a bankruptcy filing (Sections 4.2(f) and 7.2(d)); and consideration for the covenants (Sections 10.4(b) and 10.13). The Open Legal Questions give the current reading of each.

Terms in brief. Covered Facility: a data center in the largest tier of the Standards, as the Standards define it; Section 3.6(a) treats the Project as one.

Section 3.4 Outside Dates; Lapse; Tolling.

(a) Commencement. A Phase is commenced when the Developer has obtained a building permit for a Compute Hall within that Phase and has commenced vertical construction.

If a Phase is commenced but not completed by its outside completion date, its authorization does not lapse, but no later outside commencement date is extended. (c) Tolling. Outside dates are tolled day-for-day by force majeure under Section 8.3; by delay in the Interconnecting Utility’s provision of service or execution of an interconnection agreement not attributable to the Developer; by the pendency of an appeal or challenge to an Approval not brought by the Developer or an affiliate; and by any period during which a state or federal permit application is pending without action attributable to the Developer. Section 3.5 Power Usage Effectiveness.

(a) Certification. Before the first building permit for any Compute Hall in a Phase, the Developer shall deliver to the County a certification by a Florida-licensed professional engineer of record stating for that Phase (1) the Design Peak Power Usage Effectiveness, being the ratio of total facility electrical power to information-technology equipment power at the governing extreme condition under subsection (b), at full information-technology load and with the cooling plant in its design operating mode, including any redundant equipment then operating; (2) the Design Annualized Power Usage Effectiveness over a typical meteorological year for the nearest ASHRAE climatic station; and (3) the Installed IT Capacity and the critical information technology load the Phase is designed to support at the peak ratio, with the arithmetic.

(b) Governing condition. The engineer shall model the cooling plant at the 0.4% annual cooling design dry-bulb temperature with mean coincident wet-bulb and at the 0.4% annual design wet-bulb temperature with mean coincident dry-bulb, each as published in the ASHRAE Handbook (Fundamentals) for the nearest station to the Project Site, and shall use whichever condition produces the higher peak ratio, stating both results, the station, and the edition. Where the cooling architecture is wholly dry, the engineer shall so state and the dry-bulb condition governs.

(c) Capacity at the extreme condition. The Developer shall size and operate the Project so that Aggregate Infrastructure Demand does not exceed the Approved Capacity at the governing extreme condition. The certification is the Developer’s demonstration that it has done so.

(d) Lower ratio. Section 10.9 then applies, and review under Section 3.2(c) includes it. As to a standard that so applies, the Developer waives any right that may vest by equitable estoppel or otherwise at common law, which s. 163.3233(3), Florida Statutes, preserves. The same waiver applies to each rate under subsection (a)(1) and each impact fee under subsection (a)(3).

(c) Later impact fees. An impact fee ordinance under subsection (a)(3) applies to each building permit applied for after its effective date. Obligations already performed under Sections 4.2 and 4.3 and Module M2 in a fee category are credited under s. 163.31801(5)(a), Florida Statutes.

(d) Limit. This Section anticipates no other land development regulation, as to which Section 10.7 governs, and enlarges no Approved Capacity.

Section 10.9 Stricter Standard Governs.

(a) Where a standard of this Agreement, of Exhibit “O,” of Exhibit “S,” or of the County’s data center standards as they apply to the Project addresses the same subject as another of them, the stricter governs on each point, including a lower limit, a shorter averaging period, a larger interface, or an earlier deadline.

(b) No standard of this Agreement, Exhibit “O,” or Exhibit “S” is a maximum, a safe harbor, or a limit on the County’s police power as to its subject. No figure in Section 2.3, no Campus Development Envelope, no plan approval, and no phase release waives any standard, and every Phase remains subject in full to Exhibits “O” and “S.”

Section 10.10 No Enlargement of Obligations. Nothing in Sections 3.2 to 3.5, Section 4.7, or Section 10.2 enlarges any obligation of the Developer. A disposition by the County of an item acquired under Section 4.3 does not revive, enlarge, or extend any obligation of the Developer as to that item. No failure of the County to spend amounts received under Section 6.2, to revalidate Exhibit B-1, or to maintain the statement under Section 10.2(b) enlarges any obligation of the Developer.

Section 10.11 Approvals; Invalidation; Survival.

(a) Approvals. The obligations of this Agreement operate only if the Approvals are granted and the Developer then proceeds with the Project. If the Approvals are denied or never granted, this Agreement is void from the start, neither Party has any liability under it, and no vested rights arise from it.

(b) Later invalidation. If, after the Developer commences development, the Approvals or any part of them are quashed, invalidated, or set aside by a court, the invalidation operates prospectively only and does not erase accrued obligations, and the covenants of this Agreement, voluntarily granted and recorded, survive to the fullest extent severable and remain enforceable as covenants running with the Project Site, independently of the land-use entitlement. This subsection and Section 10.12 are applied together to preserve the greatest lawful measure of the protections of this Agreement, Exhibit “O,” and Exhibit “S.”

(c) Independent declaration. The declaration of restrictive covenants in this Agreement is an independent recorded instrument, enforceable on its own terms as covenants running with the land whether or not the statutory development agreement is valid, enforceable, or in effect, and whether or not the Term has expired. Each Party would have entered into the declaration independently of the Act.

(d) Independent grounds. The Approved Capacity limits rest independently on the grid, water, transportation, emergency-response, and public-safety findings of Exhibit B-1 and not on any fiscal or tax characterization. If any statute, rule, or judicial decision restricts the County’s authority to impose or enforce any provision of this Agreement, the remaining provisions continue in force, and the Approved Capacity limits survive on those grounds.

(e) Revocation. No revocation or modification of this Agreement releases a covenant that survives under this Section or Section 9.4, any security under Section 2.4(c), 4.2(f), or 7.2 or Exhibit “S,” Section S-6.1(d) or S-20.2, or any obligation already accrued, and Section 10.21 applies.

Section 10.12 Severability and Reformation.

(a) If any provision of this Agreement, Exhibit “O,” or Exhibit “S” is held invalid or preempted, the remainder continues in effect. Pending a substitute, the Developer shall comply with the closest lawful equivalent of the invalidated provision and with every related requirement that is not itself invalid, including applicable permit limits, and the invalidation of a provision does not authorize the activity it restricted except to the extent the controlling law affirmatively permits it.

(b) The Parties shall negotiate in good faith a lawful substitute that most nearly achieves the invalidated provision’s mitigation purpose and shall use diligent efforts to agree within ninety (90) days. Failing agreement, the County may adopt a conforming substitute condition for [the High-Density Compute Overlay District] through its land development regulations and public hearing process, consistent with this Agreement and applicable law. The Parties shall also meet and confer on any planning consequence of the invalidation.

(c) If a monetary covenant is held invalid under the characterization the Parties intend, it is sustained under any other lawful characterization, including as mitigation of a measured impact paid into the Mitigation Account, and is reformed only to the minimum extent necessary, preserving in all events the caps, segregation, and expenditure rules of Sections 4.1(c), 4.2, and 4.6.

Section 10.13 Covenants Running with the Land. On recording, this Agreement is a declaration of restrictive covenants. Its covenants, including those of Exhibits “O” and “S,” touch and concern the Project Site, run with it, and bind the Developer and all successors, assigns, tenants, and transferees for the life of the High-Density Compute Use. Each covenant touches and concerns the Project Site because it answers an impact of the development and operation of the Project on the Project Site and the surrounding area, burdens the Project Site, and benefits the County in its governmental capacity serving the affected area. Under s. 163.3239, Florida Statutes, the burdens of this Agreement bind, and its benefits inure to, all successors in interest to the Parties.

Section 10.14 No County Obligation or Liability.

(a) Water. The County’s provision of potable or reclaimed water is subject to applicable tariffs, water management district allocations, and availability. Nothing in this Agreement obligates the County to guarantee any quantity of water or other service or to construct any facility. The unavailability of reclaimed or potable water because of permitting, allocation, drought, or capacity is not a breach by the County, and the Project’s cooling design shall not depend on a County guarantee of supply.

(b) Utility approvals. The risk of obtaining any approval of a cooperative or municipal utility rests with the Developer. Its failure or delay is not a breach by the County and creates no obligation or liability of the County.

(c) No procurement. Nothing in this Agreement obligates the County or any other public agency to purchase, contract for, or appropriate funds for any service or asset of the Developer or of any other person, except payment for County Conduit the County requests under Section 4.5. Any other such contract is separate from this Agreement and is subject to the County’s procurement requirements and to annual appropriation.

(d) Receipt. The County’s receipt of a forwarded report or a log under Section 6.3, or of any other report or submittal under this Agreement, creates no duty of the County beyond its ordinary functions and no liability. No failure to give notice under Section 10.18(c), and no disclosure the County makes in good faith in responding to a public records request or in publishing a submittal as this Agreement or the Standards require, is a default of the County or gives rise to any liability of the County.

Section 10.15 No Joint Venture. Nothing in this Agreement creates a partnership, joint venture, or agency between the County and the Developer, or pledges or lends the County’s credit or taxing power in violation of Article VII, Section 10 of the Florida Constitution.

Section 10.16 Sovereign Immunity; Third Parties. Nothing in this Agreement, including any designation of the County as an additional insured, waives or extends the County’s sovereign immunity or the limits of s. 768.28, Florida Statutes. Except as Sections 10.20 and 10.21 provide, this Agreement creates no rights in any person other than the Parties and their permitted successors. Nothing in this Agreement limits any action that s. 163.3243, Florida Statutes, authorizes.

Section 10.17 Assignment and Transfer.

(a) Assignment. The Developer may assign this Agreement only with a transfer of the Project Site, on thirty (30) days’ prior written notice, and on delivery of the assignee’s written assumption of all obligations of this Agreement, replacement bonds, security, and insurance in the assignee’s name, and evidence reasonably satisfactory to the County of the assignee’s financial capacity to perform. The assignor is released from obligations accruing after the transfer only on the County’s written confirmation that these conditions are met. The County shall confirm, or state in writing which condition remains unmet, within [__] days after receiving the assumption, security, and evidence; absent confirmation, the assignor remains liable. Section 5.5 governs any transfer to a foreign entity or to any other person to which Section 5.5 prohibits a transfer.

(b) Partial transfer. The Developer may transfer a portion of the Project Site only by a recorded instrument, joined by the transferee and delivered to the County, that (1) allocates to the transferred portion a stated share of the Approved Capacity and of each Phase’s capacity, the shares of all owners never exceeding the Approved Capacity; (2) states which owner performs each obligation of Articles 4 and 7, Module M2, and Exhibit “S,” Sections S-12.1 to S-12.3, Part S-22, Section S-23.2, and Part S-24; and (3) binds the transferee to Exhibit “S,” Sections S-1.4 and S-25.2. Until the County confirms in writing that the allocation of obligations is adequate, the obligations of Sections 4.2, 4.3, and 7.2 and Module M2 remain joint and several among all owners. The County shall confirm, or state in writing why the allocation is not adequate, within [__] days after receiving the recorded instrument. Each owner of any part of the Project Site covenants, for the benefit of each other owner, not to seek, without the written consent of each other owner, an amendment that Section 9.2 bars or any amendment of Section 9.2 or of this sentence.

(c) Leaseholds. Where any part of the Project Site is or becomes subject to a leasehold, the Developer shall cause the lessee to execute, before it occupies or develops its premises, an acknowledgment of the recorded covenants of this Agreement. An acknowledgment is not a waiver of any right the lessee holds independently, and this Agreement does not purport to waive any right of a person who is not a party to it.

Section 10.18 Public Records.

(a) This Agreement, and every report, certification, and submittal delivered to the County under it, is a public record under Chapter 119, Florida Statutes, except information that is exempt or confidential by law.

(b) The Developer may mark any portion of a submittal it believes is exempt or confidential, citing the statute that applies, including s. 119.071(3), Florida Statutes, for security and firesafety system plans, s. 119.0715, Florida Statutes, for trade secrets, and s. 119.0725(2), Florida Statutes, for critical infrastructure and technology security information, and shall deliver a redacted copy with the marked submittal. For each portion marked as a trade secret, the Developer states why it qualifies, including the value the information derives from not being generally known and the steps taken to keep it secret.

(c) On a request for marked information, the County shall give the Developer notice promptly, and in any case within [__] business days, without delaying its response, and shall respond as Chapter 119, Florida Statutes, requires; the County’s determination governs its response. The Developer may seek relief from a court at its own cost and shall reimburse the County for any fees and costs assessed against it for withholding information at the Developer’s request. The Developer shall also reimburse the County for any judgment, settlement, or award against the County on a claim of a person other than the Developer arising from the County’s good-faith response to a public records request for information the Developer delivered, whether or not marked.

(d) No marking applies to the measured data and summary figures reported under Section 3.5(f) and Exhibit “S,” Sections S-7.4, S-9.3, S-23.1(c), and S-23.2(b), to the log under Section 6.3, or to the summary terms of each agreement delivered under Section 5.2(a) or 5.6(b), namely the tariff or rate schedule under which service is rendered, the term, whether a minimum demand or minimum bill applies, the early termination terms, and the form of security, each of which is public. Such an agreement is delivered with each customer-specific dollar amount, contract demand, and rate redacted, as Section 5.2 provides.

Section 10.19 Notices. Notices shall be in writing and delivered by hand, nationally recognized overnight courier, or certified mail to the addresses beneath the signatures, with a copy of each notice to the County also delivered to the County Attorney.

Section 10.20 Estoppel Certificates. Each Party shall, within thirty (30) days of written request, execute an estoppel certificate confirming the status of this Agreement and any default known to it. A Qualified Mortgagee, and a purchaser or assignee named in the certificate, may rely on it.

Section 10.21 Mortgagee Protections. The County shall deliver to each Qualified Mortgagee that has provided a notice address a copy of each default notice when it is given to the Developer. Each Qualified Mortgagee has the Developer’s cure period plus an additional thirty (30) days to cure; for a non-monetary default the additional period shall not exceed ninety (90) days beyond the Developer’s cure period, plus, where possession is required to cure and the Qualified Mortgagee is diligently pursuing foreclosure, the further time foreclosure reasonably requires, not exceeding one hundred eighty (180) days. The County shall not terminate this Agreement without first giving that notice and opportunity to cure. Section 8.4 applies.

Section 10.22 Recording. Within fourteen (14) days after execution, the County shall record this Agreement in the public records of the County, as s. 163.3239, Florida Statutes, requires.

Section 10.23 No Tax Increment Payment.

(a) The Developer shall not seek, apply for, or accept, and shall cause each affiliate under common control with it not to seek, apply for, or accept, any rebate, reimbursement, grant, or other payment measured by, calculated from, or sized by reference to tax increment attributable to the Project Site, whether a community redevelopment agency, the County, or another public body makes it. This subsection applies whether the payment is made to the Developer, to such an affiliate, or to another person for work either of them is obliged to perform or pay for, and includes a payment of increment revenue within the meaning of s. 163.340(22), Florida Statutes, and a payment under an area reinvestment agreement within the meaning of s. 163.387(1)(b)1.b., Florida Statutes.

(b) This Section does not limit the Developer’s right to contest an assessment or to recover taxes paid in excess of those lawfully due, or a public body’s spending of increment revenue on facilities or services it owns or provides for public use, other than a payment described in subsection (a).

(c) A breach of this Section is a default. Where the Developer or an affiliate receives a payment this Section bars, the Developer shall repay it to the public body that made it within [__] days after its receipt.

That edition continues to govern if the standard is later revised or withdrawn. A later edition applies only by amendment of this Agreement under Section 9.2 or, under Section 10.8, as the County’s data center standards incorporate it by ordinance. The Florida Building Code, the Florida Fire Prevention Code, the uniform firesafety standards under s. 633.206, Florida Statutes, and the standards adopted in them apply as state law provides, and no edition of a named standard imposes on an energy storage system a firesafety requirement more stringent than those uniform firesafety standards.

(c) Successor bodies. The dissolution, merger, or succession of a named agency, standards body, publisher, or benchmark provider is resolved by reference to the successor performing the corresponding function or, absent one, under subsection (a) for a named standard and subsection (b) for the index.
