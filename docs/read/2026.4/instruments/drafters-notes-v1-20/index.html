FLORIDA MODEL INSTRUMENTS FOR DATA CENTER DEVELOPMENT

Drafter's Notes

Prepared by In-Kind Standard · Citizen-prepared working draft · Not legal advice.

Known gaps. No county has adopted the instruments these notes explain, and no licensed attorney has reviewed them. None of the local studies their figures need has been done.

These notes are keyed to the section numbers of the model instruments. For each instrument they list the blanks and choices an adopter completes, what sets each one, and the statute that requires the provision where one does. They are not part of any instrument and add no requirement. Each instrument also opens with a heading block, and most carry short drafting notes at the clauses they concern; both are removed before a draft prepared from the instrument is first made public.

How to read the tables.

  • "Basis to be recorded by the County" means no basis is stated for the figure. The County builds its own record before the hearing.
  • The figures shown in brackets are placeholders. None is a recommendation, and none should be carried into an instrument without a basis of its own. A figure marked [insert] has no default.
  • The last column names the statute that requires the provision or fixes the figure. Otherwise it says who decides: "County choice" or "Parties' negotiation." "Study figure" means the figure comes from the study the instrument names (the impact fee Technical Study, or Exhibit "B-1" and the Impact Findings), and the Board sets it on the record within the range the study supports.
  • Names, dates, ordinance and recording numbers, and other fill-ins that only record facts are not listed.
  • "Std." means Exhibit A, Data Center Standards. "B-" means a section of Exhibit B, the Data Center Technical Manual.

Case names. Court decisions whose captions name a private individual or an official are cited by court, year and subject, with the reporter or docket citation where one is given; a caption naming an entity on one side may be cited by the entity's name alone, as the 1988 PW Ventures decision is. Six U.S. Supreme Court decisions keep their usual names, because the tests they set are known by those names: 483 U.S. 825 (1987); 512 U.S. 374 (1994); 570 U.S. 595 (2013); 601 U.S. 267 (2024); 430 U.S. 705 (1977); and 576 U.S. 155 (2015).

1. Model Data Center Standards Ordinance, Exhibit B and Module M1

The adopting ordinance adds Exhibit A, Data Center Standards, to the land development code as a chapter, adds a data center entry in three tiers to the use table, and adopts Exhibit B, the Technical Manual, by reference. Exhibit A classifies data centers by size and sets the standards a Tier 3 data center (a Covered Facility) must meet; Exhibit B holds the submittal contents, test methods and report formats. Module M1 is optional: it adds Sec. 15A, a conditional use review for Tier 2 and Tier 3 data centers, for a county that does not rely on its existing special exception or conditional use procedure.

Cautions.

  • Section 28 of Chapter 2025-190, Laws of Florida, bars every Florida county (each is listed in DR-4806, DR-4828 or DR-4834; confirm the County's listing) from proposing or adopting a more restrictive or burdensome land development regulation, or review procedure, before 1 October 2027, and s. 252.422, Florida Statutes, imposes the same bar for one year after a hurricane makes landfall, in each county listed in a federal disaster declaration that lies at least partly within 100 miles of its track. Measure the 100 miles from the nearest part of the county to the track while the storm was a hurricane. Whether an emergency declaration counts under s. 252.422(1) is open; the cautious reading is that it does. Get the County Attorney's written determination before any notice (Recital 7; kit Part E).
  • Post the business impact estimate on the County's website no later than the date notice is published (s. 125.66(3)). The adopting ordinance changes a use list, so it takes two advertised hearings under s. 125.66(5)(b), after referral to the local planning agency under s. 163.3194(2).
  • Commission the acoustic, hydrogeologic and cost studies that support the bracketed figures before the hearing. The water use limit, the fuel storage cap and the per-megawatt assurance amount are marked [insert] or left blank and have no default.
  • Keep the adoption under Part II of Chapter 163, as Section 2 provides; the exemptions in ss. 57.112(6) and 125.675(5)(g)1., Florida Statutes, cover ordinances adopted under that part, but they turn on substance: a challenger is most likely to call Sec. 12(d), the out-of-County disposal covenant in Sec. 13(a), and the Sec. 17 operational rules something other than land development regulation. Neither exemption reaches fees or a claim under Section 28(3) of Chapter 2025-190, s. 252.422(4), s. 70.001 or s. 70.45.

3. Model Data Center Public Safety and Emergency Power Impact Fee Ordinance

This ordinance charges each Covered Facility an impact fee under the Florida Impact Fee Act for fire rescue and hazardous materials capital facilities and, where its Technical Study finds need and benefit, for emergency medical services and emergency power at County Emergency Facilities. It is adopted by its own ordinance, separate from the standards, and may be adopted as a division of an existing impact fee chapter. Category C and Sec. 4.2 are the fee half of Module M2.

Cautions.

7. Model Community Benefits Agreement and Clause Bank

The Model Community Benefits Agreement is a private contract between the developer and an existing community organization. The County is not a party and has no role, and the agreement is not a condition of any approval (Sections 2.1 and 2.2); the County's standards bar the County from considering it (Std. Sec. 16). The Clause Bank holds optional terms, each inserted at the number it bears; every bracketed figure in either is set by the parties' negotiation, and none is a recommendation.

Cautions.

  • County staff and commissioners do not refer community groups to the developer, attend or facilitate negotiation, review drafts, or describe the agreement in a staff report (see Std. Sec. 16(b) and the certifications in kit Part A, Sections 12(a) and 13).
  • County staff and commissioners take no part in the annual meeting under Section 4.5. A commissioner who attends does so as a member of the public and does not discuss with another commissioner any matter that may come before the Board (s. 286.011). While a quasi-judicial application on the Project is pending, a commissioner who attends may receive an ex parte communication; where the County has adopted the procedure of s. 286.0115(1), it is not presumed prejudicial if its subject and the identity of the person with whom it took place are disclosed and made part of the record before final action. No decision-maker relies on the agreement or its performance in a decision (Std. Sec. 16(b), (d)), and a Party need not answer a question from a member, officer or employee of a Public Approver while an application by or for the Developer concerning the Project is pending before it (Section 4.5(d)).
  • The contacts under Section 4.5(e) answer questions about the agreement. A report that a commitment of the Developer is not being met goes to the report contact under Section 4.5(h), which logs and answers it without asking for or recording who made it; Section 4.5(h) requires the form to collect no identifying or device data; check the default logging of the form's host and of any analytics, security or delivery service in front of it. A complaint about the facility's compliance with a County standard goes to the operator's complaint contact under Std. Sec. 17(b) and (c), or directly to the County (Std. Sec. 17(e)).
  • Each party publishes the full agreement, with every exhibit and the completed Module form, before the first public hearing (Section 4.1(a)); neither files it in the County's application file (Section 2.2(a)).
  • A Clause Bank term marked "[Terms to be drafted]" cannot be used until it is written.
  • Set the Fund Holder's acceptance period (Section 5.8(b)) so the holding form is known before the Grant Policy deadline (Section 5.11(a)) and the first valuation (Section 5.6(b)).
  • Section 12.6 declares the commitments of Articles V to IX, and the Developer's duties that support them, to be covenants running with the Project Site. Whether the supporting duties (reporting, the annual meeting, Monitoring Fund and custody payments, business interruption cover, assumption by later ground lessees and operators, the no-subsidy terms and any Legacy Reserve) bind a successor in title that has not assumed the agreement is Open, leaning no (Open Legal Questions, Q26; Counsel 26.2). The assumptions under Sections 12.3 and 12.7 bind transferees, ground lessees and operators either way.
