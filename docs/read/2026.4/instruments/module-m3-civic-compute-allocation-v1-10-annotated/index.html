FLORIDA MODEL INSTRUMENTS FOR DATA CENTER DEVELOPMENT

Module M3. Civic Compute Allocation

Model form. Remove this heading block, and every drafting note, before the draft is first made public: by the County when the Module is attached to a lease of County land, and by the parties when it is attached to a private agreement.

What this is. Module M3 states the Civic Compute Allocation: a share of the facility's computing capacity delivered in kind to a public or nonprofit Recipient, never to the County. A Board attaches it to the ground lease as rent in kind (Part 2; Lease Exhibit "G"). Under the private agreement, Parts 1 and 3 and Schedule 1 apply once the community organization elects capacity in kind, and some other provisions apply from signing under every election (Part 3; its Section 5.4).

What it does. The Developer delivers the Allocation without charge, scaled to Installed IT Capacity (Section 1.4), to an institution that already operates computing, which offers it free to users for public-interest uses under an acceptable-use policy that reserves a share for outside users and bars surveillance of the public (Section 1.5). Undelivered capacity is paid for in money held by an independent Custodian or, after a finding of low demand, in another in-kind benefit, and a letter of credit or surety bond secures the obligations (Sections 1.15 to 1.20). No approval is conditioned on the Allocation, and the County's only role is as landlord under a lease (Section 1.3).

Works with. Under the lease, the rent in kind is in addition to cash rent of not less than fair market rental value, even while a reset cap holds Base Rent below it (Sections 2.2, 2.4 and 2.12(5)), and Section 2.12 changes named lease provisions. Under the private agreement, the Developer's commitment is paid as money unless the community organization (the Community Party) elects computing for all or part of it (its Sections 5.1 to 5.5). The optional Sections 2.5 (County computing share) and 3.6 (Instruction Share) and paragraphs T-12 (procurement through the Developer) and T-15 (retired equipment) are published in the Module M3 Options.

Before use. Under a lease, the Board sets each bracket, including the ratio R, in the minimum lease terms of the solicitation, names the Custodian and two Host Institutions before the solicitation, with their forms of acceptance in hand (Section 2.1(c)), and includes in the award record the items Section 2.12(16) lists. Under a private agreement, the parties negotiate the brackets.

Known gaps. No county has adopted this model, and no licensed attorney has reviewed it. Each bracketed figure is a placeholder that no study supports, and no engineer has reviewed the cloud form's conversion (paragraph T-13(c)). Some provisions rest on law that is not settled: directing rent in kind to a nonprofit Recipient (Section 2.2(c)); whether the duty to deliver the Allocation under a private agreement binds a later owner or ground lessee that has not assumed it (Section 1.4; private agreement Section 12.6); a public host's power to agree to the dedication and transfer of hardware (Section 1.6(d)); "other consideration" under s. 350.81, Florida Statutes, at a governmental host (Section 1.5(g)); sales tax where Recipient-Owned Hardware is housed on site (paragraph T-17(e)); sanctions screening through institutions (paragraph D-5(c)); and the Developer's bankruptcy (Sections 1.20, 2.2(a) and 2.3). The Open Legal Questions give the current reading of each.

Terms in brief.

  • "Abandonment": a long cessation of substantially all compute operations (lease Section 1.1, from development agreement Section 7.3; private agreement Section 1.1).
  • "Distress Event": a shorter cessation, a receivership or a power cutoff for nonpayment (the same sections), with the failures Section 1.2 adds.
  • "Barred Person": a person the private agreement's Section 1.1 makes ineligible, such as a Public Approver.

PART 1. COMMON TERMS

Section 1.1 Application.

(a) This Module applies where (i) the Board of County Commissioners takes part of the rent under a ground lease made on the Model Ground Lease as rent in kind (the "Lease"), or (ii) a community benefits agreement made on the Model Community Benefits Agreement attaches or applies provisions of this Module under its Section 5.4 (the "Agreement"). The Lease or the Agreement is the "Instrument."

(b) Part 1 and Schedules 1 to 3 apply under either Instrument. Part 2 applies only under a Lease, and Part 3 only under an Agreement. Where Part 2 or Part 3 states a term for a matter, that term governs under its Instrument. Where a provision of this Module states a specific term, including a cure period, a Substitute Benefit or a Conversion Floor, it governs over a general term of the Instrument.

(c) The obligations of this Module are covenants of the Instrument. The Instrument cites a provision of this Module with the word "Module," for example "Module Section 1.4" or "Module paragraph T-3."

(d) A term the Instrument defines, including "Approvals," "Approved Capacity," "Compute Hall," "Operations Commencement Date," "Phase," "Project," "CPI-U" and "Abandonment," has the meaning the Instrument gives it, except as Section 1.2 provides. Section 1.4 The Allocation.

(a) Amount. From the first delivery date and for the Term, the Developer shall make the Allocation available to the Recipient in kind, without charge for the capacity itself. The Allocation, in megawatts, is A = R x C, where R is the ratio the Board states in its published minimum lease terms or, under an Agreement, the ratio in Section 5.1(a) of the Agreement, and C is Installed IT Capacity in megawatts, measured under paragraph T-2. Expressed as an annual quantum, the Allocation is A multiplied by 8,760 hours.

(d) First delivery. First delivery shall occur not later than [__] months after the Operations Commencement Date or, under an Agreement, the Operations Commencement Date of the first Phase.

(e) Scaling. The Allocation scales with Installed IT Capacity by its own terms as the Project grows, is restated at each Phase, and applies to the Project as enlarged without amendment of this Module.

(f) Maximum. The Allocation is the Developer's maximum in-kind computing obligation under the Instrument. The value of the Allocation, or of a component, for any period is its Replacement Cost: the price the Recipient would pay an unaffiliated provider, at prevailing market rates, for committed, reserved capacity equivalent in compute, accelerator class, memory, bandwidth and storage, of the then-current hardware generation consistent with paragraph T-8, with the availability and non-preemption of paragraph T-3, for the same quantum under Section 1.4, under a commitment for the period valued or, where no published rates or arm's-length transactions exist for a commitment of that length, for the nearest length for which they exist, adjusted under paragraph V-3, together with the power, cooling, network and support attributable to it. Replacement Cost is never measured by spot, preemptible or promotional rates, by salvage or book value, by the Developer's internal cost, by any nominal or per-unit buyout price, or by the rates of a provider that offers them only to a defined community, sets them with public or philanthropic support, or cannot supply the quantum valued.

V-3 Evidence. The valuer relies on published rates and arm's-length transactions for committed capacity of comparable class, quantity and term, adjusted for differences in generation, availability and term, and states each source and adjustment. A published price index is used only for the market segment, pricing basis and commitment length it measures, and the valuer states that basis.

V-4 Timing. A valuation is made before the security under Section 1.20 is first delivered, each year by [date], and within [__] days after any Conversion Event, the start of a payment period under Section 1.9(c) or 1.15(c), or notice of an uncured shortfall under Section 1.16.

V-5 Valuer panel. Before first delivery, the Developer and the Recipient agree in writing a pre-qualified panel of not fewer than [three (3)] independent firms with demonstrated experience pricing enterprise compute capacity, none engaged by the Developer, its affiliates or the Recipient within the prior [__] years other than as valuer under this Schedule. They review the panel every [__] years and replace any firm that ceases to qualify. If they do not agree a panel within [__] days, or if no Recipient has accepted when a valuation is due, the Appointing Authority names the firms.

V-6 Selection and cost. For each valuation, the Developer and the Recipient jointly select the valuer from the panel. If they do not agree within [__] days, or there is no Recipient, [the panel firms serve in rotation in the order listed]. The Developer pays the valuer at customary rates.

V-7 Data access. The Developer furnishes the valuer and the Recipient, under reasonable confidentiality terms, the installed-capacity, configuration and utilization data reasonably necessary to apply this Schedule, and keeps books and records sufficient for that purpose, subject to independent audit at the Recipient's request [paid through the Monitoring Fund under Section 1.21]. The valuer may receive trade secrets in confidence, and they are not published. Where the Recipient is subject to Chapter 119, Florida Statutes, the Developer may designate trade secrets as s. 119.0715, Florida Statutes, provides, and data the valuer needs but the Recipient does not is furnished to the valuer only.

V-8 Report. The valuer delivers a written valuation to the Developer, the Recipient (or, while there is none, the Custodian) and the Counterparty stating the value, the method, the sources and adjustments under paragraph V-3, [any Conversion Floor computation,] and, for a shortfall, the value of a conforming Allocation, the value of the Allocation delivered, and the difference.

V-9 Objection; final-offer selection.

(a) The Developer or the Recipient may object within [thirty (30)] days after delivery. Each then submits its final figure, with workpapers, to a second valuer jointly selected from the panel (or, failing agreement, chosen under paragraph V-6), who selects the figure closer to the Replacement Cost the second valuer finds on the valuation, the submissions and this Schedule, and no other figure, and delivers, within [__] days after the last submission, a written decision stating its reasons. The selection is final and binding as to amount. The objecting party bears the second valuer's cost unless its figure is selected.

(b) [Reserved.]

(c) While there is no Recipient, the Custodian may object and submit a figure under subsection (a) in the Recipient's place, its reasonable costs of doing so being paid [through the Monitoring Fund under Section 1.21] [by the Developer, including any second-valuation cost subsection (a) places on the Custodian].

V-10 Interim payment. Pending an objection, the Developer pays the undisputed amount, which is not less than the greater of [the Conversion Floor, where Section 1.18(a) applies it, and] the most recent final valuation, trued up with interest under Section 1.18(d) within [thirty (30)] days after resolution.

V-11 Other disputes; publication. Any dispute under this Schedule other than amount, including panel formation, data access, scope, or a claim that a selection resulted from fraud, partiality or failure to follow this Schedule, is resolved under Section 1.25. Valuation amounts, any Conversion Floor computation and the methodology are published under Section 1.22; configuration-level workpapers and unit-pricing inputs may be redacted.
