EXHIBIT “B-1”: IMPACT, NEXUS, AND PROPORTIONALITY FINDINGS

Outline and Methodology

Model form. Remove this heading block, and every drafting note, before the County first makes the draft public.

What this is. A model-form drafting guide: the structure and method by which each adopting county prepares its own Exhibit “B-1,” the impact, nexus and proportionality findings on which the mitigation in a host community development agreement rests. Exhibit “B-1” is not Exhibit B, the Data Center Technical Manual.

What it does. Parts 1 to 5 state what Exhibit “B-1” must do, its structure, the method rules, the order of preparation and the common failure modes. The obligations Agreement Section 4.1(a) names, such as the Physical Infrastructure Mitigation Payment, rest on the findings, as does the County purchase option (Agreement Section 7.4(d)). The Board sets each figure on the record within the range the findings support, and where they support none, none is due (1.5).

Works with. Agreement Section 4.1 governs Exhibit “B-1.” Most inputs come from filings the Ordinance requires, with the contents its Technical Manual (Exhibit B) sets (Part 4). An adopted impact fee, including under the companion public safety and emergency power impact fee ordinance, governs its category (1.6). A ground lease of County land takes its Impact Findings (its Exhibit “B,” Part 2) from findings prepared as Agreement Section 4.1 provides. Exhibit “S” is the schedule of supplemental operating covenants an Agreement or lease may attach.

Before use. Set aside each category an adopted impact fee covers (1.6), and settle which optional parts of the Agreement the findings must support, such as Module M2 (B-1.3.9) or the County purchase option (B-1.3). The County selects and directs the preparer (B-1.8). Derive every figure from the County’s own record (1.4).

Known gaps. No county has adopted this model, and no licensed attorney has reviewed it. No Exhibit “B-1” has yet been prepared on this method. Findings under it support Agreement terms that rest on law that is not settled: emergency power at a public shelter (B-1.3.9) and the liquidated amounts for sound violations (B-1.3.11). The Open Legal Questions give the current reading of each.

Terms in brief.

  • Approved Capacity and Phase: the maximum Aggregate Infrastructure Demand and critical IT load the Agreement authorizes, and each part of the Project the Agreement states, or adds by amendment, as a Phase (Agreement Section 1.1).
  • Abandonment: the cessation of substantially all compute operations for the period Agreement Section 7.3(a) states.
  • Connected Compute Load and Site Electrical Capacity: the Ordinance’s measures of nameplate computing capacity and of the rated capacity of every source of supply (Sec. 2).
  • AID, Aggregate Infrastructure Demand; PSC, Public Service Commission; IRP, integrated resource plan; WMD, water management district; BESS, battery energy storage system; CPI-U, the price index of Agreement Section 1.1.

PART 1: WHAT EXHIBIT “B-1” HAS TO DO

Exhibit “B-1” is the evidentiary foundation of every monetary and in-kind obligation in the Agreement. Seven functions, and it fails if it does not perform all seven.

1.1 Essential nexus. Each obligation must connect to an impact the Project actually causes. Nollan v. California Coastal Commission, 483 U.S. 825 (1987). Exhibit “B-1” supplies the connection by identifying the impact before the remedy.

1.2 Rough proportionality. Each obligation must be roughly proportional in nature and extent to the impact. Dolan v. City of Tigard, 512 U.S. 374 (1994). Individualized determination is required; a category-wide assumption is not enough. In Florida, s. 70.45, Florida Statutes, gives a property owner a claim against a condition that “lacks an essential nexus to a legitimate public purpose and is not roughly proportionate” to the impacts of the proposed use, with the burden of proof on the government.

1.3 Monetary obligations are covered. Koontz v. St. Johns River Water Management District, 570 U.S. 595 (2013). The Physical Infrastructure Mitigation Payment, the First Responder amounts and every other monetary obligation are within the doctrine whatever they are called.

1.4 Legislative adoption does not lower the standard. Sheetz v. County of El Dorado, 601 U.S. 267 (2024), holds that the Takings Clause does not distinguish between legislative and administrative permit conditions. It leaves open whether a condition imposed on a class of properties must be tailored with the same degree of specificity as one that targets a particular development; an Agreement for one Project is the second kind. The model-form problem follows from 1.2 and this holding: a figure carried unchanged from another county’s agreement weakens the individualized showing for every county using it. No number in any model instrument may travel. Each adopting county derives its own.

1.5 It sets and caps every figure. The Board sets each figure in the Agreement on the record within the range this Exhibit supports, and where this Exhibit supports no figure, none is due (Agreement Section 4.1(c)). The low end is the hardest to challenge, because the County must show that each obligation is proportionate to the impact (s. 70.45, Florida Statutes); a higher figure needs a record that supports it. Under Section 4.1(c), a Payment that would exceed the impact values quantified in the most recently adopted Exhibit “B-1” is reduced to that ceiling automatically, without election, notice, or amendment. Section 6.2(a) caps compliance monitoring reimbursement at the lower of the stated annual amount and the cost quantified here. Exhibit “B-1” is not background material. It is the number that controls payment, and it operates whether or not anyone invokes it.

1.6 It measures only what no fee already covers. Where the County has adopted an impact fee under s. 163.31801, Florida Statutes, for a category of capital facilities, including under the companion data center public safety and emergency power impact fee ordinance, the fee governs that category (Agreement Section 4.7(b)). Mark each such category “covered by impact fee” and derive no rate for it. Obligations that are not capital facilities, such as training, maintenance and fuel, remain measurable here. The fee rests on its own technical study for the whole use class; do not merge the two.

1.7 It measures impacts, not benefits. A computing allocation, a workforce program or any other community benefit that answers no measured impact does not belong in this Exhibit or in the Agreement. Those items sit, if anywhere, in a private agreement to which the County is not a party, and the County neither measures nor values them.

PART 2: STRUCTURE

B-1.1 Baseline Conditions

Pre-Project condition of each affected system, dated, with the source of each measurement. Without a baseline there is no marginal impact, only a total, and a total is not attributable to the Project.

State all three capacity measures separately. Do not collapse them.

The physical-infrastructure categories divide by Aggregate Infrastructure Demand, not by computing load. Grid, water, wastewater, transportation, emergency response, stormwater, and noise impacts are driven by total facility demand. Dividing them by critical IT load produces a rate that under-recovers precisely as cooling load rises, which is to say a facility with worse power usage effectiveness generates more impact and pays less per unit. Section 1.2(b) of the Agreement states the same rule from the other direction.

B-1.4 Per-Unit Derivation Table

Each category total divided by its stated denominator. Every line states its denominator on its face. This table is the operative output. It is what makes the obligations scale automatically on a capacity change without amendment.

B-1.5 Rate Expression

Derive the rate only from impacts on public infrastructure the County owns or operates (for example B-1.3.3, B-1.3.4, and B-1.3.10), net of Agreement Section 2.4 and of anything supporting Section 4.3 or Module M2 (Sections 4.2(a) and 4.7(e)). The grid categories B-1.3.1 and B-1.3.2 support the findings for Article 5, Exhibit “S,” Parts S-22 and S-25, and Section 10.11(d), not the Payment, unless the County itself owns or operates the serving electric system, in which case net them as B-1.3.1 provides.

State the rate for the Physical Infrastructure Mitigation Payment as a dollar amount per Megawatt of Aggregate Infrastructure Demand, unless this Exhibit shows that a different measure better fits a particular impact, in which case state that measure for that impact (Agreement Section 4.2(b)). State the range the study supports, from its low end to its high end; the Board sets the rate within it (1.5). The Agreement indexes the rate by CPI-U under Section 4.2(d), so this Exhibit states the rate as of its own date and does not index it.

The measure matters as much as the form. Section 4.2(b) applies the rate to the capacity authorized or certified for each Phase. A rate stated against a different measure cannot be applied, and the automatic reduction of Section 4.1(c) then compares unlike quantities.

B-1.6 Revalidation Protocol

Section 4.1(d) of the Agreement requires revalidation by a qualified professional at each update of the decommissioning plan under Ordinance Sec. 14(a), on any amendment increasing the Approved Capacity, and at any other time the County elects. The Developer reimburses the cost of each revalidation at a plan update or on an amendment increasing the Approved Capacity and, where the County has kept the bracketed option in that Section, of any other revalidation the County elects. A revalidated Exhibit “B-1” is adopted by the Board after a public hearing and governs prospectively from its adoption. A failure to revalidate does not increase any obligation of the Developer, and the most recently adopted Exhibit “B-1” continues to bound every obligation it supports until replaced. Under Section 4.2(e) a revalidated Exhibit sets the rate, up or down, for every installment of a Phase not yet commenced, always within the range it supports and never above the impact values it quantifies; a Phase already commenced keeps its rate unless the revalidation lowers it. Neither Party may rely on an Exhibit “B-1” the County has not adopted. State here who prepares each revalidation.

B-1.7 Source Register

Every input on its own line with a source flag: verified (a document or study in the record), estimated (derived by stated method from verified inputs), or placeholder (no basis yet). A placeholder is acceptable in a draft and fatal in an executed instrument. The register is what allows a reviewer to test the Exhibit without redoing it.

B-1.8 Limitations and Preparer Qualifications

Who prepared each section, their qualifications, what was not studied, and why. The County selects and directs the preparer, who answers only to the County, and selects it under its procurement code and, where the work includes professional services, s. 287.055, Florida Statutes, which may be through a continuing contract. Where the County has adopted Sec. 15(f) of the Ordinance (Section 4A of its adopting ordinance), the requester’s deposit pays for the first preparation, and the findings may be used only for the Agreement, for the impact findings of any lease of County land for the Project, and for the County’s own later studies, and are not considered in deciding any application or making any determination under the Ordinance; otherwise the Developer reimburses the first preparation under Agreement Section 4.1(d) if the Agreement takes effect, and the County bears it if the Agreement does not take effect (Agreement Section 10.11(a)).

B-1.9 Exhibit “S” Covenants

Section 10.5 of the Agreement requires a finding for each covenant of Exhibit “S” the Agreement attaches: that the covenant bears an essential nexus to, and is roughly proportional to, the part of a Project impact that the standards of the Ordinance leave unanswered at the Project Site. For each covenant, state the impact and the B-1.3 category that measures it, the section of the Ordinance that addresses the same subject and why it leaves part of the impact unanswered at this site, the measure of that part, and how the covenant’s figure is sized to it. Where the Ordinance answers the impact in full, record that finding (3.4); the covenant is then left out of Exhibit “S” or adopted in the Ordinance for every facility.

PART 3: METHOD RULES

3.1 Cost causation, not cost allocation. The question is what the Project causes at the margin, not what share of an existing system it uses.

3.2 Marginal, not average. Average cost per customer is not an impact finding.

3.3 Net out what is paid elsewhere. Where a utility tariff under s. 366.043, Florida Statutes, already recovers the cost from the large-load customer, the same cost cannot appear in Exhibit “B-1.” Where an adopted impact fee covers a category, that category is excluded (1.6). Where the County funds capital facilities in a measured category in part from ad valorem taxes, non-ad valorem assessments, or a dedicated sales tax, net out the revenue the Project will contribute toward those facilities through those sources, computed from the Project’s own projected tax and assessment base and not from an average per unit of other development, so that the Project does not pay twice. Double counting is the fastest way to lose a proportionality challenge, and it is the most likely error in the grid categories. Where the serving utility is a rural electric cooperative under chapter 425 or a municipal utility, s. 366.043 does not apply; net against the contract or service agreement by which the applicant bears connection and incremental infrastructure cost, and say so, because the absence of a tariff is not the absence of recovery.

3.4 Document the zeroes. A category studied and found to produce no material impact is a finding and strengthens the Exhibit. Silence looks like avoidance.

3.5 Present value conventions. State the discount rate and the horizon once, apply them everywhere, and disclose both, with the source of the rate (for example, the Treasury rate for the maturity closest to the horizon). Show the effect of reasonable alternative rates and state which discount rate the preparer recommends and why. The Board chooses the figure within the resulting range on the record.

3.6 Nothing is derived from what any party wants. The Exhibit is built from impacts upward, states the range the study supports, and leaves the choice of a figure within it to the Board. Where the study supports none, the figure stays blank and nothing is due. Drafting toward any desired number, higher or lower, defeats the Exhibit as evidence.

3.7 State the denominator on every derived rate. A rate carried from one denominator to another is a category error, and it produces an answer that is wrong in a direction no reviewer can see from the face of the table.

3.8 Answer each impact once. For each impact measured, the record states whether it is answered by a standard of the Ordinance, by an item in the Agreement, by an impact fee, or not at all with the reason (In-Kind Eligibility and Characterization Test, 5.3). No impact supports two obligations (Agreement Section 4.7(e)).

3.9 Value each item two ways. For each item of the Agreement that answers an impact, other than land or an interest in land, state its burden value, measured as the In-Kind Eligibility and Characterization Test, 5.4, provides (for construction, equipment, or a service, the Developer’s documented cost of providing it plus its forgone sales, net of any reimbursement, revenue, or avoided cost the item produces for the Developer; for a share of output, the Developer’s marginal cost of the share plus its forgone sales, net of the same), and its recipient value, set by an independent valuer by the method that Test, 2.4, gives for the item’s type. For land or an interest in land, state its fair market value. Until the law settles which value governs rough proportionality, the item is treated as roughly proportional only if it is proportional at the higher of the two. Where an adopted impact fee applies, also state the item’s credit value, which serves the credit only. The staff report records both values for each item (County Procedure and Records Kit, Part A, Section 13).

PART 4: SEQUENCE OF PREPARATION

  • B-1.2 denominators, since everything divides by them, and there are three.
  • B-1.1 baselines, which take the longest to assemble and gate everything else.
  • Identify the categories an adopted impact fee covers, and set them aside.
  • B-1.3.1 and B-1.3.2, the grid categories, which are the largest values and carry the double-counting risk.
  • B-1.3.3 and B-1.3.4, water.
  • B-1.3.13, monitoring, which is small but unlocks a provision already drafted at Section 6.2(a).
  • Remaining categories.
  • B-1.4, B-1.5, B-1.6, and B-1.9 for each Exhibit “S” covenant the Agreement will attach.
  • B-1.7 source register, maintained throughout rather than assembled at the end.

PART 5: COMMON FAILURE MODES

  • Carrying a figure from a model form. Nothing in the record ties it to the Project’s impacts, and adopting it by ordinance does not change that (1.2, 1.4).
  • Deriving a physical-infrastructure rate against computing load.
  • Double counting grid costs already in a tariff, or assuming no recovery exists because no tariff does.
  • Deriving a rate for a category an adopted impact fee already covers.
  • A rate stated against a measure other than the one Section 4.2(b) applies.
  • Purpose narration inside the findings, such as describing a payment as given for the approvals, for certainty, or for vesting. Mitigation answers an impact; it is not a price.
  • Measuring a community benefit here (1.7).
  • Placeholders surviving into execution.
