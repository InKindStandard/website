ORDINANCE NO. [______]

AN ORDINANCE OF [COUNTY NAME] COUNTY, FLORIDA, ESTABLISHING IMPACT FEES FOR LARGE DATA CENTER FACILITIES TO FUND PUBLIC SAFETY AND EMERGENCY POWER CAPITAL FACILITIES; ADOPTING A TECHNICAL STUDY; PROVIDING FOR CALCULATION, COLLECTION, CREDITS FOR IN-KIND CONTRIBUTIONS, ACCOUNTING, AND USE OF FUNDS; PROVIDING FOR ADMINISTRATION AND APPEALS; PROVIDING FOR SEVERABILITY, CODIFICATION, AND AN EFFECTIVE DATE.

WHEREAS, the Board makes these findings on the basis of the Technical Study adopted in Sec. 2.1;

WHEREAS, [where the Technical Study so finds] Covered Facilities create a distinct demand on the County's public safety capital facilities, arising from utility-scale battery energy storage, large stored fuel volumes, high-voltage electrical infrastructure, and the specialized response these require, that is not measured by the floor-area or employment measures used for other nonresidential uses;

WHEREAS, [where the Technical Study so finds] Covered Facilities create a measurable demand on the County's capital facilities for emergency power at County Emergency Facilities, which the Technical Study states with its measure and its basis;

WHEREAS, the fees in this Ordinance are proportional and reasonably connected to the need for additional capital facilities created by that demand, and reasonably connected to the expenditure of the funds collected and the benefits accruing to the new construction, as s. 163.31801(4)(f) and (g), Florida Statutes, require;

WHEREAS, the fees fund capital facilities only and are not a tax, a revenue measure, or a charge for any regulatory approval;

WHEREAS, the fees are calculated using the most recent and localized data available, as s. 163.31801(4)(a), Florida Statutes, requires;

WHEREAS, the business impact estimate that s. 125.66(3), Florida Statutes, requires was posted on the County's website on [date], no later than the date on which notice of the proposed enactment was published; and

[WHEREAS, before notice of this Ordinance was first given, the County Attorney determined in writing that Section 28 of Chapter 2025-190, Laws of Florida, and s. 252.422, Florida Statutes, do not prohibit the County from proposing or adopting this Ordinance on the dates on which it was proposed and adopted, and the Board so determines; and]

WHEREAS, notice of this Ordinance was given, and the public hearing held, as s. 125.66(2), Florida Statutes, requires;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF [COUNTY NAME] COUNTY, FLORIDA:

ARTICLE I. GENERAL PROVISIONS

Sec. 1.1 Authority and Findings. This Ordinance is adopted under Art. VIII, s. 1(f) and (g) of the Florida Constitution; s. 125.01, Florida Statutes; and the Florida Impact Fee Act, s. 163.31801, Florida Statutes. It is an impact fee ordinance within the meaning of s. 163.31801 and shall be construed to comply with that section. The recitals of this Ordinance are adopted as the findings of the Board.

Sec. 1.2 Definitions. In this Ordinance:

"Aggregate Infrastructure Demand," "Approved Capacity," "Covered Facility," and "Development Unit" have the meanings given in the Data Center Standards, [Chapter / Article __] of the Land Development Code (the "data center standards"). If those standards are not in effect, they have the meanings stated in the Technical Study.

"Capital Facilities" means infrastructure as defined in s. 163.31801(3), Florida Statutes, for public facilities in a Fee Category, including fire department and emergency medical service vehicles and the equipment necessary to outfit them for official use, and excluding repairs, maintenance, operating, training, and personnel costs.

"County Emergency Facility" means (a) a fire station, emergency medical services station, emergency communications (911) center, or potable water or wastewater treatment facility that the County owns and that the Technical Study identifies; or (b) a public shelter that the County operates or designates, where the Technical Study identifies it and supports its inclusion. An emergency operations center is not a County Emergency Facility.

"Fee Category" means a category of Capital Facilities for which a fee is adopted in Sec. 3.1.

"Fee Unit" means the measure stated in Sec. 3.2 for each Fee Category.

"In-Kind Contribution" means land, construction, equipment, or other Capital Facilities in a Fee Category delivered to the County under Article IV.

"Technical Study" means the study adopted under Sec. 2.1, as revalidated under Sec. 2.3.

Sec. 1.3 Applicability. This Ordinance applies to each building permit for a Covered Facility, and for each expansion that increases the Aggregate Infrastructure Demand or a Fee Unit of a Covered Facility, for which an application is first submitted on or after the effective date, whether or not then complete. It does not apply to a permit application submitted before the effective date, whether or not then complete (s. 163.31801(4)(d)), or to reconstruction or replacement that does not increase the impact, to the extent s. 163.31801(14) applies.

Sec. 1.4 Agreements Not Considered. This Section is not waivable. In administering this Ordinance, including in calculating any fee, approving any credit, or deciding any appeal, the County shall not consider, request, require, solicit, or take into account whether the applicant has entered into, offered, negotiated, or declined any development agreement, ground lease, community benefits agreement, contribution, or other voluntary undertaking with the County or with any other person, except a credit agreement under Article IV, any credit s. 163.31801(5)(a) requires, and the application of s. 163.3233, Florida Statutes. A decision in which such consideration occurred shall be vacated and reheard on request of the applicant, or of any other person entitled to participate as a party under the County's quasi-judicial procedures, made within [thirty (30)] days after the decision is rendered, or on the Board's own motion within the same period. A request shall identify the statement, document, or other evidence, in the record or in a public record, showing the consideration. The Board shall deny, without rehearing, a request that identifies none. The Board decides a timely request at its next regular meeting held at least [__] days after it is filed, by a signed, written order filed with the Clerk of the Board. A timely request under this Section is a motion for rehearing authorized by this Ordinance, whether or not it identifies evidence. A rehearing on the Board's own motion does not postpone rendition.

Sec. 1.5 Relationship to Other Law. This Ordinance does not amend, waive, or substitute for any standard of the data center standards or any other land development regulation, and compliance with this Ordinance is not compliance with any of them. Nothing in this Ordinance regulates any matter within the exclusive jurisdiction of the Public Service Commission.

ARTICLE II. TECHNICAL STUDY

Sec. 2.1 Adoption of the Technical Study. The Board adopts the study titled [title], prepared by [preparer], dated [date], which is incorporated by reference and kept on file with the Clerk of the Board. The Technical Study is a plan-based, demonstrated-need study using the plan-based methodology s. 163.3164, Florida Statutes, defines; uses the most recent and localized data available within four (4) years of the current impact fee update; and is adopted within twelve (12) months after it was initiated, whether or not this Ordinance increases a fee (s. 163.31801(4)(a)).

Sec. 2.2 Required Contents. The Technical Study shall, for each Fee Category:

(a) identify the demand Covered Facilities create, its measure (the Fee Unit), and the data on which it rests, including County incident and response records, the fire official's assessment of the response capability that battery energy storage and stored fuel at the scale of the Covered Facilities require, and comparable data from other jurisdictions only where local data are unavailable, stated as such, and, for a public shelter, identify only the shelter demand Covered Facilities themselves create, measured as the persons the County's emergency management plan would shelter after evacuation of the area the fire official assigns to a battery, fuel or hazardous materials incident at a Covered Facility, stating that area and its basis, and not countywide shelter demand or shelter demand from a hurricane or other area-wide event;

(b) identify the Capital Facilities needed to serve that demand, their cost, and the service standard on which the need rests, tied to the County's adopted capital improvements plan, and state the forecast of data center growth on which any lumpy capital need (such as a specialized response vehicle) is spread;

(c) exclude any existing deficiency and any cost the County would incur without Covered Facilities;

(d) credit the revenue Covered Facilities will contribute toward the same Capital Facilities through other sources, including ad valorem taxes, non-ad valorem assessments, and any dedicated sales tax, computed from their own projected tax and assessment base and not from an average per unit of other development, so that no Covered Facility pays twice;

(e) state the supported range of the fee per Fee Unit, the basis on which a rate within it may be chosen, and, for rates below the top of that range, the share of the measured cost of the Capital Facilities that each would leave to be paid from general revenues;

(f) state the basis on which the funds collected will be spent on Capital Facilities that benefit the Covered Facilities that pay them;

(g) project, over a ten (10) year period, the growth of Covered Facilities and the demand they create in each Fee Category, state the anticipated capacity impacts, and list the capital projects, with their timing, that the fees will fund;

(h) state the capacity standard on which each need rests, such as apparatus or station capacity per unit of demand; and

(i) identify, for each County Emergency Facility, whether its emergency power equipment is counted in Category A, Category B, Category C, any other impact fee the County charges, or a water or wastewater capacity or connection charge, and count it in one only.

Sec. 2.3 Revalidation. The County shall revalidate the Technical Study at least every [four (4)] years and before any increase in a fee. An increase is subject to the limits and procedures of s. 163.31801(6). A decrease, suspension, or elimination rests on a revalidated Technical Study or on a finding by the Board, on the record, that the demand the Technical Study measures has fallen, except that a decrease to another rate within the range the current Technical Study supports rests on the Board's reason for that rate, stated on the record. A decrease, suspension, or elimination may take effect without the ninety-day notice that applies to a new or increased fee (s. 163.31801(4)(d)).

Sec. 2.4 No Category Without a Finding. A Fee Category is adopted only where the Technical Study finds both need and benefit for it. Where the Technical Study does not support a category, the bracket for that category in Sec. 3.1 is left blank and no fee is charged for it.

ARTICLE III. FEE SCHEDULE

Sec. 3.1 Fee Categories and Rates. The following fees are imposed on each Covered Facility subject to this Ordinance, each rate set by the Board on the record within the range the Technical Study supports:

Fee CategoryCapital Facilities fundedRate per Fee Unit
A. Fire rescue and hazardous materialsFire stations or bays, fire apparatus, hazardous materials and battery-fire response vehicles and the equipment to outfit them, training structures that are fixed capital[$______; study-dependent]
B. Emergency medical servicesEmergency medical facilities and vehicles and the equipment to outfit them[$______; study-dependent; adopt only on a finding under Sec. 2.4]
C. Emergency power at County Emergency FacilitiesGeneration, storage, switchgear, and connections installed as fixed capital at County Emergency Facilities whose emergency power the Technical Study counts in Category C under Sec. 2.2(i)[$______; study-dependent; adopt only on a finding under Sec. 2.4]

Sec. 3.2 Fee Units. The Fee Unit for each Fee Category is the measure the Technical Study adopts for it, from among the following: [megawatts of Approved Capacity first authorized for construction under the building permit, as certified by the engineer of record] [megawatt-hours of battery energy storage installed under the building permit] [gallons of liquid fuel storage capacity installed under the building permit] [another measure the Technical Study shows to measure demand better]. Where a Fee Category uses more than one measure, the Technical Study states how they combine.

Sec. 3.3 Calculation. The fee for a building permit equals the rate for each Fee Category multiplied by the Fee Units authorized or installed under that permit, less any credit under Article IV. Where Fee Units are later increased, the fee is charged on the increase.

Sec. 3.4 General Nonresidential Rates. For each rate, the Technical Study states whether it includes the demand measured by a general nonresidential rate the County charges for the same category of Capital Facilities, or is an increment above it. If it includes that demand, a Covered Facility pays the rate under this Ordinance in place of, and not in addition to, the general nonresidential rate on the same development; if it is an increment, the Covered Facility pays both. No demand is charged twice. An increment funds only Capital Facilities that the study supporting the general rate does not include; otherwise the rate is an increase subject to Sec. 2.3. A rate charged in place of a general nonresidential rate is an increase subject to Sec. 2.3 to the extent it exceeds that rate for the same Capital Facilities.

ARTICLE IV. CREDITS AND IN-KIND CONTRIBUTIONS

Sec. 4.1 Credit Required. The County shall credit against the fee for a Fee Category any contribution, whether identified in a development order, proportionate share agreement, or any form of exaction, related to public facilities or infrastructure in the general category or class of that Fee Category, including monetary contributions, land dedication, site planning and design, or construction, on a dollar-for-dollar basis at fair market value (s. 163.31801(5)(a)). No credit is given in a category for which no fee is charged (s. 163.31801(5)(b)).

Sec. 4.2 In-Kind Delivery of Emergency Power. Where Category C is adopted, an applicant may deliver, in place of all or part of the Category C fee, generation or storage designed, installed, and commissioned by the applicant at one or more County Emergency Facilities whose emergency power the Technical Study counts in Category C under Sec. 2.2(i), behind the meter serving the facility, and conveyed to the County on acceptance. At a shelter the County does not own, the equipment is delivered only under a written agreement with the owner that keeps it for the facility's emergency use and, from 1 January 2027, comports with s. 163.3177(6)(h), Florida Statutes. The credit equals the fair market value of the Capital Facilities conveyed, certified by an independent professional engineer. Maintenance, fuel, and operation are not Capital Facilities and earn no credit; an applicant may undertake them under a separate agreement. The County may not accept equipment under this Section unless the Board has identified funding for its maintenance and replacement, which may be funding the instrument requiring the equipment provides. Equipment the applicant keeps, whether leased to the County or owned by a utility, earns no credit, except that capability a development agreement required at a County Emergency Facility before the Category C fee took effect is credited as s. 163.31801(5)(a), Florida Statutes, requires, at the fair market value of the capability for the period it is provided. No delivery under this Section involves electricity from the applicant's site.

Sec. 4.3 Other In-Kind Contributions. An applicant may deliver other Capital Facilities in a Fee Category, including fire apparatus and the equipment to outfit it, where the Capital Facilities are identified in the Technical Study or in the County's capital improvements program for that category, and receive credit under Sec. 4.1.

Sec. 4.4 Credit Agreement. A credit under this Article is established by a written credit agreement, in this order:

(a) For an In-Kind Contribution, the County decides in writing whether to accept it. The County is not required to accept title to, or operation of, any In-Kind Contribution, except that it accepts in writing Capital Facilities it requires, whether in a development order, a development agreement, a proportionate share agreement, or any other form of exaction, once they meet the acceptance standards of a credit agreement under paragraph (b), so that the credit s. 163.31801(5)(a), Florida Statutes, requires is not withheld. The acceptance standards include at least the written specifications for the Capital Facilities that the credit agreement states, title free of liens and of encumbrances other than those the specifications or the County in writing permit, Sec. 4.8 where it applies, for land an environmental site assessment the applicant provides that finds no contamination, for equipment that stores fuel, a release and tightness check of its tank and piping that it has passed and an inspection that shows no release of fuel at its site that has not been cleaned up, both made at the applicant's cost, and for equipment the funding for its maintenance and replacement that Sec. 4.2 requires. The County does not require equipment in kind unless the Board has first identified that funding or the instrument requiring the equipment provides it. The County does not accept Capital Facilities that do not meet the acceptance standards, and never accepts land the assessment finds contaminated or fuel-storing equipment that has not passed the release and tightness check and the inspection for past releases.

(b) The County Administrator shall approve a written credit agreement with the applicant that meets this Article, stating the contribution and the basis on which its fair market value is determined, and, for an In-Kind Contribution the County requires or has agreed in writing to accept, the delivery date and the acceptance standards. Each credit agreement includes the terms of Secs. 4.7 and 4.8.

(c) The credit is computed ministerially under the credit agreement.

(d) A credit for an In-Kind Contribution is applied when, and not before, the County accepts the delivered Capital Facilities in writing under the acceptance standards, at its fair market value as of the date of acceptance, as an independent professional engineer or, for land, an appraiser certifies.

(e) The credit decision is subject to appeal under Sec. 6.5.

Sec. 4.5 No Credit for Required Work. No credit is given for any work, equipment, or capacity that the data center standards, another land development regulation, a permit condition, or the Florida Building Code or Florida Fire Prevention Code requires of the applicant on its own site.

Sec. 4.6 Transfer of Credits. The County is a single impact fee district for each Fee Category. Credits are assignable and transferable at any time after establishment to any development or parcel in the County that receives benefits from the improvement or contribution that generated them (s. 163.31801(10)). A credit applies only against the fee for the Fee Category in which it was established and, where Sec. 3.4 makes that fee an increment above a general nonresidential rate for the same class of Capital Facilities, against the increment first and then against that general rate.

Sec. 4.7 Accepted Capital Facilities. The County holds Capital Facilities it accepts under this Article for the purposes of their Fee Category for their useful life or, for land, for as long as the Technical Study shows it is needed for that category. The net proceeds of any earlier sale or other disposal are deposited in that Fee Category's accounting fund under Sec. 5.1, used only under Sec. 5.2, and are not fees for the purposes of Sec. 5.3.

Sec. 4.8 Construction on Public Property. For an In-Kind Contribution constructed or installed on land the County or another public body owns or leases, the applicant shall: (a) award the work of each project whose estimated cost exceeds the applicable amount in s. 255.20(1), Florida Statutes, as adjusted under s. 255.20(2), without dividing a project to come under that amount, to an appropriately licensed contractor, by sealed bids, to the lowest qualified and responsive bidder, or by a publicly advertised request for proposals or request for qualifications, in each case under solicitation and award procedures the County approves in writing as following those its ordinance sets for its own construction contracts; (b) require the contractor to execute and record the payment and performance bond s. 255.05, Florida Statutes, requires, naming the County and any other public body that owns the land as additional obligees, and to deliver a certified copy of the recorded bond to the County before work begins; and (c) execute and record any bond s. 255.05 requires of the applicant itself. Meeting this Section is an acceptance standard of each credit agreement.

ARTICLE V. ACCOUNTING AND USE OF FUNDS

Sec. 5.1 Separate Accounting Funds. The County shall deposit fees for each Fee Category in a separate accounting fund for that category (s. 163.31801(4)(b)). The funds are not part of the general fund and shall not be transferred to it.

Sec. 5.2 Use of Funds. Funds in each accounting fund shall be earmarked and used only to acquire, construct, or improve Capital Facilities in that Fee Category that serve the demand of Covered Facilities, as the Technical Study identifies (s. 163.31801(4)(h)). Funds shall not be used for any cost the definition of Capital Facilities excludes, for any existing deficiency, or to pay existing debt or for previously approved projects unless the expenditure is reasonably connected to, or has a rational nexus with, the increased impact generated by Covered Facilities (s. 163.31801(4)(i)). Funds are spent on Capital Facilities at a shelter the County does not own only under a written agreement with the owner, including any interlocal agreement, that keeps them for the facility's emergency use, states who holds title to them and, from 1 January 2027, comports with s. 163.3177(6)(h), Florida Statutes.

Sec. 5.3 Spending Period; Refund. Fees shall be encumbered for Capital Facilities in their Fee Category within [__] years after collection, the period the Technical Study's capital plan supports. Funds in each accounting fund are deemed encumbered and spent in the order collected. Fees not so encumbered shall be refunded, with the interest they earned, to the owner of record of the Covered Facility on written application made within [__] days after notice of eligibility.

Sec. 5.4 Reporting. The County shall account for and report fee collections and expenditures as s. 163.31801(4)(b), (8), and (13) require. Each year the County shall also publish, in plain language, the amount collected and spent in each Fee Category, the Capital Facilities acquired or built, with the location of each and the area it serves, and each credit granted.

ARTICLE VI. GENERAL ADMINISTRATION

Sec. 6.1 Administration. The County Administrator or designee administers this Ordinance, calculates each fee, and decides each credit under the Technical Study and this Ordinance.

Sec. 6.2 Collection. The fee is due at, and not before, issuance of the building permit for the work that creates the Fee Units (s. 163.31801(4)(e)). No building permit shall issue until the fee is paid or a credit under Article IV is applied.

Sec. 6.3 Independent Fee Calculation. An applicant may submit an independent fee calculation, prepared by a qualified professional using the methodology of the Technical Study and data specific to the Covered Facility. The County Administrator shall accept it where it is supported by competent substantial evidence and shall state any rejection and its reasons in writing.

Sec. 6.4 Administrative Charges. Any charge for collecting the fee is limited to the County's actual costs (s. 163.31801(4)(c)).

Sec. 6.5 Appeals. An applicant may appeal a fee calculation, the rejection of an independent fee calculation, or a credit decision to [the Board / a special magistrate] within [thirty (30)] days of the written decision. The applicant may pay the fee under protest and obtain the building permit while the appeal is pending.

Sec. 6.6 Burden of Proof. In any action challenging a fee or a credit under this Ordinance, the County carries the burden of proving by a preponderance of the evidence that the fee or credit meets the requirements of state legal precedent and s. 163.31801, and no deferential standard applies (s. 163.31801(9)).

Sec. 6.7 Overpayment. A written request for refund or credit of an alleged overpayment is decided as s. 163.31801(15), Florida Statutes, as created by Chapter 2026-161, Laws of Florida, provides: the County shall approve or deny the request within thirty (30) days, and shall provide any refund or credit within thirty (30) days after the County receives the payor's election.

ARTICLE VII. SEVERABILITY; CODIFICATION; EFFECTIVE DATE

Sec. 7.1 Severability. If any provision or application of this Ordinance is held invalid, the remainder continues in effect. If a Fee Category is held invalid, the other Fee Categories continue to operate independently. This Ordinance is adopted separately from the data center standards, and its invalidity does not affect them.

Sec. 7.2 Codification. This Ordinance shall be codified with the County's other impact fee ordinances.

Sec. 7.3 Effective Date. This Ordinance takes effect [date], which is not less than ninety (90) days after its adoption (s. 163.31801(4)(d)).

ADOPTED by the Board of County Commissioners of [COUNTY NAME] County, Florida, this ___ day of ________, 20__.

ADOPTION AS A DIVISION OF AN EXISTING IMPACT FEE CHAPTER

A County whose impact fee chapter already contains general administration provisions may adopt this Ordinance as a division of that chapter, on these terms:

(a) The County may omit any section of Article VI listed in the table below, but only where county counsel confirms in writing that the existing chapter meets s. 163.31801, Florida Statutes, on the subject stated beside that section. A listed section whose subject counsel has not so confirmed, and every other section of this Ordinance, is adopted as written.

(b) Each omitted section is replaced by one line citing the provision of the existing chapter that covers its subject.

(c) Sec. 7.2 reads: "Sec. 7.2 Codification. This Ordinance is codified as Division [__] of [Chapter __] of the County Code. A reference in this Ordinance to an omitted section means the provision of that chapter cited in its place. Where this Ordinance and that chapter differ on the same subject, this Ordinance governs."

Section that may be omittedSubject the existing chapter must cover, meeting s. 163.31801, Florida Statutes
Sec. 6.1 AdministrationAdministration of the fees, calculation of each fee, and decision of each credit
Sec. 6.2 CollectionCollection no earlier than issuance of the building permit (s. 163.31801(4)(e))
Sec. 6.3 Independent Fee CalculationAn applicant's independent fee calculation, accepted where supported by competent substantial evidence, with written reasons for any rejection
Sec. 6.4 Administrative ChargesCollection charges limited to actual costs (s. 163.31801(4)(c))
Sec. 6.5 AppealsAppeal of a fee calculation, the rejection of an independent fee calculation, or a credit decision, with payment under protest so the building permit may issue during the appeal
Sec. 6.6 Burden of ProofThe County's burden of proving the fee or credit by a preponderance of the evidence, with no deferential standard (s. 163.31801(9))
Sec. 6.7 OverpaymentWritten approval or denial of a request for refund or credit of an overpayment, and the refund or credit, within the periods of s. 163.31801(15), Florida Statutes, as created by Chapter 2026-161, Laws of Florida
