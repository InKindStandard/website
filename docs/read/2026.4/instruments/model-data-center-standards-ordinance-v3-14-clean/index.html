Sec. 1. Purpose and Applicability.

(a) Purpose. This Chapter sets standards for the siting, design, operation, monitoring, and decommissioning of data centers to protect the public health, safety, and welfare.

(b) Applicability. This Chapter applies to each Covered Facility for which a complete application for a development order is filed on or after the effective date, and to each expansion of an existing facility that brings it into Tier 3 or increases its capacity while in Tier 3. Secs. 13, 17, and 18 apply to every Covered Facility, whenever approved, beginning [one hundred eighty (180)] days after the effective date. Sec. 3 and Sec. 4(c) apply to every application for a data center, in any tier.

(c) Tier 2 data centers. Where the County has adopted Sec. 15A, Secs. 4 through 14, other than Sec. 11A, and Secs. 15(b), 15(c), 17, and 19 apply to a Tier 2 data center as they apply to a Covered Facility, and Sec. 15A governs its review.

(d) Vested rights. Nothing in this Chapter impairs rights vested under s. 163.3233(1), Florida Statutes, under a development agreement executed before the effective date, for the duration of that agreement, except where the agreement specifically anticipates and provides for this Chapter or its amendments under s. 163.3233(2)(c), Florida Statutes, in which case this Chapter applies as the agreement provides, upon the hearing and determination that s. 163.3233(2) requires. The Board finds that Secs. 13, 17, and 18 are essential to the public health, safety, and welfare, and Secs. 13, 17, and 18 shall apply to a development that is subject to a development agreement, as subsection (b) provides. The County may apply them to a development otherwise vested upon making the determinations and holding the hearing that s. 163.3233(2), Florida Statutes, requires.

(e) Relationship to other law.

(1) State and federal requirements, including the Florida Building Code, the Florida Fire Prevention Code, Department of Health rules, chapter 373 water law, chapter 403 environmental law, and district environmental resource permitting, are minimums. Where this Chapter is stricter and not preempted, this Chapter governs.

(2) Nothing in this Chapter amends the Florida Fire Prevention Code except as s. 633.202, Florida Statutes, permits, requires of an energy storage system a firesafety standard more stringent than the uniform firesafety standards under s. 633.206, Florida Statutes, amends the Florida Building Code, grants or denies any consumptive use permit, regulates the consumptive use of water that s. 373.217, Florida Statutes, reserves to Part II of chapter 373, Florida Statutes, regulates any matter within the exclusive jurisdiction of the Public Service Commission or a water management district, or restricts any fuel source in violation of s. 366.032, Florida Statutes. The definitions in Sec. 2, the Development Unit rules, and Sec. 10 apply without regard to fuel or generating technology.

(3) Nothing in this Chapter requires any state or federal permit or approval as a condition of processing or issuing a development permit or development order (s. 125.022(4), Florida Statutes).

(f) No exaction; fees. This Chapter imposes no exaction, dedication, impact fee, or payment to the County other than cost-based application, inspection, and administration fees established by resolution, and shall be so construed. Each resolution setting a fee, an escrow amount, or a coordinated review rate under this Chapter shall comply with s. 125.022(6), Florida Statutes. Those fees, the escrow deposit under Sec. 15(b), and any coordinated review agreement under Sec. 15(d) pay only the County's cost of reviewing and monitoring the applicant's own application and facility, and the deposit under Section 4A of the ordinance adopting this Chapter (Sec. 15(f)) pays only the County's cost of preparing the findings on a development agreement the depositor has asked the County to consider. None of them is a contribution or an instrument described in Sec. 16, and none creates an entitlement to any determination. Financial assurance under Sec. 14 secures the operator's own obligations and is not a payment, fee, or revenue of the County. A rate, fee, or charge of general application that the County imposes as the operator of a utility, including for reclaimed water service, is not a payment this Chapter imposes.

(g) Same terms. Every requirement of this Chapter, and every fee, service, and form of relief available under it, applies or is offered to every applicant on the same terms.

(h) Not a moratorium. This Chapter is not a moratorium, does not suspend the acceptance or processing of applications, and shall not be administered to produce that effect.

(i) No alternative compliance. No standard of this Chapter may be met by alternative compliance, except by an existing facility under Sec. 19(d).

(j) Referenced standards. Each named technical standard in this Chapter and the Technical Manual means the edition in effect on the effective date of this Chapter, stated as "([edition])" where the standard is cited, and that edition continues to govern if the standard is later revised or withdrawn. A later edition applies only when the Board adopts it by ordinance. The Florida Building Code, the Florida Fire Prevention Code, the uniform firesafety standards under s. 633.206, Florida Statutes, and the standards adopted in them apply as in effect from time to time.

(k) Technical Manual. The Technical Manual, adopted by reference by Ordinance No. [______] and on file with the Clerk of the Board and the [planning department], is part of this Chapter. Each submittal, study, test, plan, report, and measurement this Chapter requires shall be prepared, performed, and filed as the Technical Manual provides.

Sec. 2. Definitions.

In this Chapter:

"Aggregate Infrastructure Demand" means the total electrical power drawn by a facility, or projected in an application or authorized by a development order to be drawn, measured as the highest average demand over any fifteen (15) minute interval, including information-technology equipment, mechanical cooling, and all ancillary loads, from all sources of supply, including utility service, on-site generation of any kind, and battery energy storage discharge, without regard to the number of service points, meters, or accounts.

"Approved Capacity" means the maximum Aggregate Infrastructure Demand authorized for a Covered Facility by its development order and by any applicable overlay or zoning approval, expressed in megawatts. It is an authorization ceiling and not a measure of demand actually drawn. Where no development order states a figure, the Approved Capacity of a Covered Facility is its Site Electrical Capacity. Where a development agreement defines Approved Capacity differently, a Covered Facility subject to both measures the thresholds of this Chapter by Aggregate Infrastructure Demand alone.

"Archaeological site" means a location that contains material remains of past human activity at least [fifty (50)] years old.

"Area to be disturbed" means each area of ground disturbance that a site plan application proposes.

"BESS" means a battery energy storage system.

"Burial ground" means a place where human remains are buried, whether or not the burial is marked, including an unmarked human burial as s. 872.05, Florida Statutes, defines it.

"Cemetery" has the meaning given in s. 497.005, Florida Statutes, and includes a cemetery no longer in use.

"Certified Recycler" means a recycler holding a current certification named in the Technical Manual.

"Common control" exists where a person, directly or indirectly, holds [twenty-five percent (25%)] or more of the voting interests, capital, or profits of another; where the same person or persons hold such an interest in two or more applicants; where two or more entities share a common parent, manager, managing member, or general partner; or where a written or oral agreement, option, right of first refusal, lease, development agreement, shared-infrastructure arrangement, or established course of dealing gives one person the practical ability to direct the development of land held by another. Common control is determined by substance and not by form.

"Connected Compute Load" means the aggregate nameplate electrical capacity of information-technology equipment installed, or authorized by a development order to be installed, for computation, storage, or data transmission, measured at rated equipment input, without regard to the source of supply, the ownership of the equipment, the identity of any tenant or licensee, or the utilization actually achieved.

"Covered Equipment" means servers and server blades; microprocessors, graphics processing units, tensor processing units, and other compute accelerators; data-storage devices and arrays; networking and interconnection hardware; rack power-distribution and uninterruptible-power units; and internal liquid-cooling assemblies and their chemical compounds, in each case decommissioned, failed, or removed from service at a Covered Facility. It excludes building systems, exterior BESS, and hazardous waste governed by part IV of chapter 403, Florida Statutes.

"Covered Facility" means a Tier 3 data center under Sec. 3(a)(1).

"Data-Bearing Media" means any component capable of storing information.

"Data center" means a use whose primary function is the housing and operation of information-technology equipment for computation, storage, or data transmission, including a computing facility or colocation campus, whatever its computational architecture, processor type, cooling technology, or commercial purpose, and including any successor technology serving a substantially similar function.

"Development Unit" means all land that (1) is under common ownership or common control at the time an application is filed, or was under common ownership or common control at any time within [thirty-six (36)] months before the filing; (2) lies within [one (1) mile] of any other land meeting clause (1); and (3) is used, proposed to be used, or subject to a pending application for a data center. Land conveyed before the filing to a purchaser for value who is not under common control with the applicant, and who did not acquire it as part of an arrangement described in Sec. 3(e), is excluded.

"Emergency" means (1) an interruption of utility electric service to the Covered Facility, or an imminent interruption declared by the serving utility; or (2) a written direction of the serving utility or the applicable reliability coordinator, during a North American Electric Reliability Corporation Energy Emergency Alert Level 3 event or under an order issued under section 202(c) of the Federal Power Act, to reduce the facility's load on the grid. Participation in a demand-response, load-flexibility, or load-management program is not an Emergency.

"End user" means a person that holds, under an executed lease, license, build-to-suit, capacity, or service agreement, rights to [__] percent or more of the Connected Compute Load projected at full build-out.

"Ground disturbance" means clearing, grubbing, grading, excavation, filling, trenching, or boring.

"Mechanical Load Component" has the meaning given in ANSI/ASHRAE Standard 90.4 ([edition]).

"Projected cone of depression" means the area, delineated as Technical Manual Sec. B-8.3 provides, within which a withdrawal is projected to lower aquifer water levels by at least the drawdown that Section states.

"Qualified Refurbisher" means a public school district, state college or university, governmental entity other than the County or a department, agency, or dependent special district of the County, or Florida nonprofit corporation or cooperative association exempt under s. 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, that refurbishes or redistributes equipment for public, educational, or community purposes and is registered under Sec. 13(d).

"Sensitive Receptor" means the property line of any parcel, outside the site, in any zoning district, containing a dwelling unit, a licensed child care facility, a public or private school serving grades K through 12, a hospital or licensed health care facility, a nursing home or assisted living facility, a place of worship, a public park, or a licensed campground. A Sensitive Receptor exists from the earlier of the date the use is established and the date a building permit or development order is issued for it.

"Site Electrical Capacity" means the aggregate rated capacity of all sources of electrical supply serving a site, including utility service entrance capacity, on-site generation of any kind, and battery energy storage discharge capacity, measured at the point of common coupling.

"Sound Contour" means the area outside the site that Technical Manual Sec. B-9.4(d) maps in the most recent acoustic study or update under Sec. 9(e).

"Technical Manual" means the Data Center Technical Manual adopted as Exhibit B to Ordinance No. [______], as amended by ordinance.

"Tier 1," "Tier 2," and "Tier 3" have the meanings given in Sec. 3(a).

"Verified Sanitization" means sanitization or destruction of Data-Bearing Media under the standard named in the Technical Manual, evidenced by a serialized per-device certificate, performed before the media leaves the facility.

"Water Usage Effectiveness" means annual site water consumption for cooling, in liters, divided by annual information-technology equipment energy, in kilowatt-hours.

Other terms have the meanings given in the Land Development Code or, where undefined there, their ordinary meaning.

Sec. 3. Use Classification.

(a) Tiers. A data center is classified in the highest of the following tiers whose threshold its Development Unit meets.

(1) Tier 3 (Covered Facility). A data center whose Development Unit has (A) an Aggregate Infrastructure Demand of [fifty (50)] megawatts or more; (B) a Connected Compute Load of [forty (40)] megawatts or more; (C) a Site Electrical Capacity of [fifty (50)] megawatts or more, where a data center is the principal use of the Development Unit; or (D) a large-scale data center as defined in s. 373.203(4), Florida Statutes. The criteria are alternatives. A data center meeting any one is in Tier 3, and the criterion first met governs the date of applicability.

(2) Tier 2. A data center not in Tier 3 whose Development Unit has a Connected Compute Load of [five (5)] megawatts or more.

(3) Tier 1. Any other data center.

(b) Where allowed.

(1) A Covered Facility is allowed only as a [special exception / conditional use] in the [__] districts, as the use table provides, upon approval under Sec. 15.

(2) A Tier 2 data center is allowed as the use table provides[; where Sec. 15A is adopted, it is a conditional use under Sec. 15A].

(3) A Tier 1 data center is allowed as the use table provides.

(c) Measurement. The tier thresholds apply to the Development Unit and not to a single parcel, building, meter, service point, phase, tenant, or entity.

(d) Aggregation. Two or more applications for a data center on land within one Development Unit, filed within [thirty-six (36)] months of one another, shall be aggregated and reviewed as one facility. Where land now in a Development Unit was approved when the Development Unit met no tier threshold to which this Chapter applies, and a later application brings it within one, the later application shall be reviewed as to the whole Development Unit. No completed construction shall be required to be modified except as Secs. 9(d), 13, 17, and 18 require.

(e) Anti-circumvention. No division or replatting of land; formation, dissolution, merger, or reorganization of an entity; separation or reconfiguration of electrical service; sequencing, withdrawal, or refiling of applications; or creation of leasehold, condominium, or licensee interests shall be given effect to place a facility outside this Chapter, or in a lower tier, where the substance of the development is a single facility.

(f) Development Unit determination. For each application for a data center, the County Administrator or designee shall make a written Development Unit determination, within the period s. 125.022(2)(b)1., Florida Statutes, allows for the completeness review, stating the land included, identified by parcel, the criteria applied, and the measured values relied upon. An instrument by which a person holds or controls included land is described only by its type, such as an option, lease, or management agreement. The determination shall be made solely on the criteria of this Section, is subject to the County's ordinary administrative appeal, and shall be included in the record of any proceeding on the application.

(g) Limited effect. The Development Unit rules govern only the tiers under this Chapter. They do not determine classification under s. 212.08(5)(r), s. 366.043, or s. 373.203, Florida Statutes, which apply their own aggregation rules.

Sec. 4. Application Requirements.

(a) Required submittals. An application for a Covered Facility shall include the following, with the content and at the time the Technical Manual, Part B-4, states:

(1) the ownership and Development Unit disclosure under subsection (c);
(2) the utility capacity letters under subsection (d);
(3) the cooling design demonstration and the comparison of cooling architectures under Sec. 7(a), and the heat rejection plant design data under Sec. 7(e);
(4) where reclaimed water is proposed, the reclaimed water documentation under Sec. 7(b);
(5) the water service terms, drought documents, and curtailment demonstration under Sec. 7(f);
(6) where groundwater or surface water withdrawal is proposed, the studies under Sec. 8(b) and, for a surface water withdrawal, the showing under Sec. 8(g);
(7) the acoustic study under Sec. 9(e);

B-11A.5 requires, none of which states a location more precise than the parcel. No finding under this Section rests on a location withheld from the County's file.

(f) Land already disturbed. This Section applies only to ground disturbance after its effective date.

Sec. 12. Hazardous Systems and Emergency Planning.

(a) BESS. BESS installations shall be sited not less than [one hundred (100)] feet from any property line and [three hundred (300)] feet from any residential structure or Sensitive Receptor, and shall meet Technical Manual Sec. B-12.1.

(b) Fuel storage.

(1) Aggregate on-site liquid fuel storage shall not exceed [insert] gallons without a written finding of necessity by the County Administrator. The County Administrator shall make the finding where a Florida-licensed professional engineer certifies, and the County's reviewing engineer accepts in writing, that the storage above that volume is no more than is needed to run the facility's standby generation at its design emergency load for [__] hours, or for the run time the Florida Fire Prevention Code or a state or federal permit requires for the facility, whichever is longer, and the storage meets paragraph (2).

(2) All fuel storage shall be either within secondary containment sized to [one hundred ten percent (110%)] of the largest single tank, with sufficient freeboard to contain precipitation where the containment is open to rain, or in a double-walled tank whose outer tank holds the full capacity of the inner tank and whose interstice is monitored; shall be set back not less than [two hundred (200)] feet from a Sensitive Receptor; and shall be outside any wellfield protection zone.

(3) Before a certificate of occupancy, the applicant shall file the spill prevention and emergency response plans Technical Manual Sec. B-12.2 requires.

(c) Emergency planning. Before a certificate of occupancy, the operator shall deliver to the fire department the pre-incident plan Technical Manual Sec. B-12.3 requires. The operator shall participate in a joint exercise with the fire department not less than [annually].

(d) Chemicals and fluids. The operator shall not use firefighting foams or heat-transfer or dielectric fluids containing intentionally added PFAS where a commercially available alternative exists, and shall file the chemical inventory and annual certification Technical Manual Sec. B-12.4 requires.

Sec. 13. Equipment Stewardship.

(a) Disposal. No Covered Equipment shall be disposed of in any landfill, transfer station, or municipal solid waste stream in the County. As a condition of the development order for a Covered Facility, and as a covenant of the operator as to its own conduct, the operator shall not cause or permit the disposal of Covered Equipment decommissioned at that facility in any landfill, transfer station, or municipal solid waste stream, wherever located. This subsection regulates the conduct of a person operating a Covered Facility in the County and does not regulate any facility, transaction, or person outside the County. Compliance is shown by the routing records under subsection (e).

(b) Routing. Within [one hundred eighty (180)] days of decommissioning, Covered Equipment shall be routed, at the operator's sole election, through one or more of: (1) a Certified Recycler; (2) a documented manufacturer takeback, exchange, or warranty program; (3) verified resale or redeployment into functional secondary use; or (4) donation or below-market transfer, as-is and without warranty, to a Qualified Refurbisher, accompanied by Verified Sanitization certificates. Nothing in this Section requires the donation pathway or designates or prefers any recipient.

(c) Data security. No Data-Bearing Media shall leave a Covered Facility by any pathway without Verified Sanitization. Media that cannot be verifiably sanitized shall be physically destroyed on site and routed under subsection (b)(1) or (2). An operator may exclude Data-Bearing Media from the donation pathway entirely. A donating operator's responsibility for a donated item ends at transfer, and the recipient assumes all risk, if the sanitization certificates were delivered.

(d) Registry. The County shall maintain, without fee, a ministerial registry of Qualified Refurbishers as Technical Manual Sec. B-13.2 provides. The County shall not designate, prefer, or limit refurbishers. The registry does not create, restrict, or adjudicate any right of first refusal, priority, or other contractual right that an operator grants voluntarily to any person in a development agreement, lease, or other instrument, and no such right is a designation or preference by the County.

(e) Records. The operator shall report routing in the annual compliance report and retain certificates as Technical Manual Sec. B-13.3 requires.

Sec. 14. Decommissioning and Financial Assurance.

(a) Plan. With the first site plan application, and updated every [five (5)] years, the operator shall file a decommissioning and adaptive-reuse plan addressing removal of BESS, fuels, coolants, and hazardous materials; demolition or reuse of structures; and stabilization and revegetation.

(b) Financial assurance.

(1) Before the first building permit for any compute hall, the operator shall deliver, and shall then maintain, a performance bond, irrevocable letter of credit, or cash escrow with a bank or trust company under an agreement the County Attorney approves, in the amount of a Florida-licensed professional engineer's estimate of decommissioning and restoration cost net of salvage value (salvage credited not below zero), or $[______] per installed megawatt, whichever is greater, plus, where Sec. 8 applies, the claims amount under paragraph (5). No parent guaranty or self-bond qualifies. The engineer shall be independent of the operator. The assurance shall be recertified upon any capacity increase and at each plan update. In this subsection, "installed megawatt" means a megawatt of Aggregate Infrastructure Demand of the phases in operation or under construction.

(2) The per-installed-megawatt amount shall be adjusted on each anniversary of the effective date by the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers published by the United States Bureau of Labor Statistics, or its successor index. No adjustment shall reduce the amount below the amount in effect for the preceding year, and the amount remains flat during any deflationary period until positive inflation resumes. The adjusted amount applies at each recertification.

(3) The estimate shall state the cost of compliance with Sec. 13, including routing, transport, and Verified Sanitization, as a gross line item, and that cost shall not be reduced by any salvage credit. Any salvage credit exceeding [__] percent of the gross estimate shall be supported by a written appraisal, prepared within twelve (12) months of the certification by a person qualified to value the equipment class, and salvage shall be credited at the lower of the appraised value or the engineer's estimate.

(4) The County may draw on the assurance only to perform unfinished decommissioning obligations or to pay a well owner claim under Sec. 8(e) that the applicant has not paid when due. The draw may be made when an amount remains unpaid [thirty (30)] days after it was due, or an obligation remains unperformed [sixty (60)] days after performance was due. Notice is given where the law permits it, but is not a condition of the draw. Within [ninety (90)] days after a draw for decommissioning, the County shall solicit the work, and shall begin it within [twelve (12)] months unless a court order or pending permit prevents it. Drawn funds are held in a separate account applied only to the work or claim for which they were drawn. Within [thirty (30)] days after any draw, the operator shall restore the assurance to the amount this subsection requires, less any drawn funds the County still holds, unspent, for the work or claim for which they were drawn. No delay by the County releases or reduces the assurance or any obligation of the operator. The County shall release the assurance upon completed restoration and payment of each well owner claim under Sec. 8(e) made before then.

(5) The claims amount is the total that the hydrogeologic study under Sec. 8(b) states under Technical Manual Sec. B-8.5. The operator shall deliver the assurance for it not later than the date the withdrawal begins, if that is earlier than paragraph (1) requires. It is restated with each study filed under Sec. 8(b), and paragraph (2) adjusts it as it adjusts the per-installed-megawatt amount. Where Sec. 8(e)(4) ends paragraphs (1) to (3) of Sec. 8(e), the claims amount becomes the amount of the claims made before then that remain unpaid, and secures only those claims. Nothing in this subsection limits, conditions, or suspends a withdrawal, or the date on which one may begin, and no failure to deliver, maintain, or restore the claims amount is enforced by curtailing, conditioning, or suspending a withdrawal of water.

(c) Abandonment.

(1) The operator shall notify the County in writing within [__] days after the principal use ceases, unless the use has resumed within that period.

(2) Cessation of the principal use for [three hundred sixty-five (365)] consecutive days without an inactivity plan approved under subsection (d) is abandonment. Upon abandonment, the decommissioning plan becomes enforceable and the assurance may be drawn as subsection (b)(4) provides.

(3) The operator shall commence decommissioning within [__] days after abandonment and complete it within [__] months after abandonment. The County Administrator may extend either period for good cause shown, by a written decision that states the cause. The operator shall post each such decision on the project page under Sec. 17(b), and the County shall publish it when it is issued.

(d) Inactivity plan.

(1) An operator may file an inactivity plan with the County Administrator not later than [__] days before the end of the period stated in subsection (c)(2).

(2) The County Administrator shall approve or deny the plan in writing within [__] days after it is filed, stating the reasons for any denial. The County Administrator shall approve a plan that (A) states the date by which the principal use will resume, not later than [__] months after the use ceased; (B) provides for the security, maintenance, fire protection, and stormwater management of the site, and for the safe storage or removal of BESS, fuels, coolants, and hazardous materials, during inactivity; and (C) keeps the assurance under subsection (b) in force.

(3) An approved plan ends on the resumption date it states. If the principal use has not resumed by that date, the use is abandoned on that date or at the end of the period stated in subsection (c)(2), whichever is later.

Sec. 15. Review.

(a) Procedure. An application for a Covered Facility is decided under the County's [special exception / conditional use] procedure in [Section __] of the Land Development Code[, or, where the County has adopted Sec. 15A, under Sec. 15A,] and under this Chapter.

(b) Independent technical review.

(1) Before an application is deemed complete, the applicant shall deposit into an escrow account, in an amount the County establishes by resolution, funds sufficient to pay the County's reasonable cost of independent technical review of the application.

(2) The County selects and directs each consultant. The applicant has no role in selection, scope, direction, or communication with a consultant, except to respond to requests for information.

(3) The applicant, and after approval the operator, shall replenish the escrow on [thirty (30)] days' notice. Until it is replenished, review of the application pauses and no review or decision period under this Chapter runs. Before the notice period ends, the applicant shall either replenish the escrow or extend the time for decision, by a written agreement under s. 125.022(2)(b)4., Florida Statutes, for the period of the pause, which the County Administrator shall enter into on the applicant's request. Where the applicant does neither, the County decides the application within the period s. 125.022(2)(b), Florida Statutes, allows, on the record then before it. The escrow funds review under Secs. 4(d), 5(b), 5(e), 7(e), 7(f), 8, 9(e), 11(c), 11A, 12(b)(1), and 15(c), and post-approval compliance monitoring, annual verification, complaint response, and independent testing under Secs. 9, 17, and 18.

(4) Any unused balance is refunded within [__] days after the final decision on the application or its withdrawal. After approval, the County funds the post-approval work listed in paragraph (3) from deposits it requires under that paragraph and refunds any unused balance of them within [__] days after decommissioning under Sec. 14 is complete.

(5) The deposit is a requirement for a complete application and is not a condition of approval.

(c) Cumulative impact assessment.

(1) The applicant shall submit a cumulative impact assessment, with the scope Technical Manual Sec. B-15.1 requires, where the application would bring the aggregate Approved Capacity of all Covered Facilities in the County to [one hundred fifty (150)] MW or more, or where any other Covered Facility is located within [five (5)] miles of the site, within the same springshed or recharge basin, or served by the same substation or transmission circuit.

(2) The assessment shall be prepared without regard to common ownership. Facilities under separate ownership are included where they meet paragraph (1).

(3) The findings on the application shall state whether the standards of this Chapter remain adequate at the cumulative level assessed. The County may impose a condition addressed to cumulative effect that the application considered alone would not warrant only where the assessment shows that, at the cumulative level, (A) sound at a shared Sensitive Receptor would exceed a limit of Sec. 9(a); (B) aggregate electrical demand, or water or wastewater loading, would exceed the capacity stated in the providers' letters under Sec. 4(d); (C) combined cones of depression would reach a well or spring outside the cone projected for the application alone; (D) traffic would exceed the County's adopted level of service standard on a road segment; or (E) aggregate emergency response demand, including simultaneous generator operation and aggregate fuel and BESS storage, would exceed the capability the fire service provider having jurisdiction states in writing. A condition shall go no further than needed to bring the cumulative result within the limit, capacity, or standard exceeded or, under clause (C), to extend Sec. 8(d) and (e) to the wells within the combined cone, and shall not require a payment, dedication, contribution, or conveyance. The findings shall also state whether combined surface water withdrawals would draw a source below a minimum flow or level the water management district has adopted under s. 373.042, Florida Statutes. That finding is not a ground for a condition, and the County shall send it to the district when it renders its decision on the application.

(d) Coordinated review.

(1) At an applicant's written request, and at its cost under a fee or cost-recovery agreement at rates the Board sets by resolution, the County may assign dedicated staff or a coordinator to the application, consolidate the applicant's concurrent applications for different approvals into one review track, hold pre-application and interagency conferences, and give factual letters confirming zoning status, entitlement status, concurrency status, and compliance history to a utility, a water management district, or a state permitting agency. Sec. 3(d) applies in every case.

(2) Coordinated review does not shorten or extend any review period, give any application priority over another, or delay an application whose applicant has not asked for it. Staff assigned under this subsection are added to, and not drawn from, the staff reviewing other applications.

(3) A factual letter states facts only. The County makes no commitment to support, advocate for, or refrain from opposing any application before another agency, and no letter is an endorsement.

(4) No decision under this Chapter may turn on whether an applicant has a coordinated review agreement.

(e) Sec. 15A. Sec. 15A applies only where the Board adopts it by a separate section of an ordinance. If Sec. 15A is not adopted, is repealed, or is held invalid in whole or in part, the rest of this Chapter continues in full force; each reference in it to Sec. 15A or to a Tier 2 data center is of no effect and shall be disregarded, the remaining text being given its ordinary meaning without that reference; and the Development Unit rules apply to the Tier 3 thresholds alone.

(f) Development agreement study. Section 4A of the ordinance adopting this Chapter governs a request that the County consider a development agreement for a Covered Facility, including the deposit for the County's impact, nexus, and proportionality findings.

(g) Later site plans. In this subsection and Secs. 5(h) and 9(e), a later site plan is a site plan for a Covered Facility filed after the decision under Sec. 15(a). [Option A: A later site plan for a Covered Facility is decided by [the planning commission / the Board] and is not designated for administrative review and approval by local government staff under s. 163.3169(1)(q), Florida Statutes.] [Option B: A later site plan for a Covered Facility that conforms to the approval under subsection (a) is designated for administrative review and approval by local government staff under s. 163.3169(1)(q), Florida Statutes, where it also meets the other terms of that paragraph.] Approval of a later site plan, including a deemed approval under s. 125.022(1)(d)2., Florida Statutes, does not relieve the operator of any requirement of this Chapter, including Sec. 9(e) and (f).

Sec. 16. Agreements Not Considered; Rehearing.

(a) Not waivable. This Section is not waivable by the applicant, by the County, or by agreement.

(b) Agreements not considered. In administering this Chapter, including in making any determination under Sec. 3 or Sec. 19, in deciding any application, and in granting any reduction, finding, alternative compliance, or extension this Chapter permits, the County shall not consider, request, require, solicit, or take into account whether the applicant has entered into, has offered to enter into, has negotiated or discussed, or has declined to enter into any development agreement, host community development agreement, ground lease, community benefits agreement, contribution, or other voluntary undertaking with the County or with any other person. The County shall not condition any approval or determination, or its timing, or the application of any standard of this Chapter, on any such instrument, and shall not delay or expedite any determination because of one. This Chapter neither requires any such instrument nor conditions anything on one. No County officer or employee acting as such shall refer any person to the applicant, or the applicant to any person, for the purpose of any such instrument to which the County is not a party, attend or facilitate the negotiation of one, review its drafts, or describe it in a staff report, agenda item, or application form, except to record its receipt, filing, disclosure, or disposition, without stating its terms, as subsection (d) contemplates.

(c) Rehearing. A decision or determination in which such consideration occurred shall be vacated and reheard on request of the applicant or of any other person entitled to participate as a party under the County's quasi-judicial procedures, made within [thirty (30)] days after the decision is rendered, or on the Board's own motion within the same period. A request shall identify the statement, document, or other evidence, in the record or in a public record, showing the consideration. The Board shall deny, without rehearing, a request that identifies none. The Board decides a timely request at its next regular meeting held at least [__] days after it is filed, by a signed, written order filed with the Clerk of the Board. A timely request under this subsection is a motion for rehearing authorized by this Chapter, whether or not it identifies evidence. A rehearing on the Board's own motion does not postpone rendition.

(d) Record. Nothing in this Section authorizes the omission, alteration, or withholding of any record. Receipt, filing, retention, or publication of a copy of an agreement described in this Section, as chapter 119 or s. 286.0115, Florida Statutes, requires, is not consideration of it. All communications remain subject to chapter 119, Florida Statutes, and any such consideration appearing in the record is evidence that this Section was violated. A statement in the record that a person or public body is a party to, or holds an interest under, an agreement described in this Section is not consideration of that agreement, provided that no decision-maker relies on the agreement's terms in any decision under this Chapter, other than as subsection (e) allows.

(e) Default under a County development agreement. Where a development agreement to which the County is a party provides for a written denial of an application for the development it governs, as a remedy for the other party's uncured default under that agreement in an obligation that mitigates an impact of that development, subsection (b) does not bar consideration of that default, or of the terms of the agreement that state the obligation and the remedy, in deciding whether to make the denial or in the denial; that consideration does not violate this Section, and subsection (c) does not apply to it. The denial is made after the notice and cure that agreement requires and within the periods s. 125.022, Florida Statutes, allows; it states the default, the notice and the cure period, and is without prejudice to a new application once the default is cured. Except as to consideration of that default and those terms, subsections (b) and (c) apply to the denial and to the decision whether to make it, and no denial may rest on any other term of any agreement.

Sec. 17. Monitoring, Reporting, Public Information, and Complaints.

(a) Annual compliance report. The operator of each Covered Facility shall file an annual report demonstrating compliance with Secs. 5 through 14, with the content and in the human-readable and machine-readable formats Technical Manual Sec. B-17.1 provides. The report may be consolidated with any report required under a development agreement or lease. It is due within [ninety (90)] days after the close of each calendar year, the first report being due for the first calendar year in which the facility operates for any part of the year. The County shall publish each report within [thirty (30)] days of filing, except any portion lawfully exempt from disclosure.

(b) Project page. From the first application for a Covered Facility until decommissioning under Sec. 14 is complete, the applicant, and after approval the operator, shall maintain a public project web page. The page shall state the approvals and permits issued, a current contact available at all hours for complaints, and the current status of construction and operation, and shall link to the sound dashboard under Sec. 9(c), the acoustic study under Sec. 9(e) and each update of it, each commissioning test report under Sec. 9(f), the generator testing schedule under Sec. 10(c), and each annual compliance report.

(c) Complaints. The operator shall accept complaints by telephone, by electronic form, and by mail, including anonymous complaints. It shall acknowledge each identified complainant within [__] hours, investigate, and give a written response stating its findings and any corrective action within [__] days.

(d) Complaint log. The operator shall keep a complaint log, which omits each complainant's personal information unless the complainant consents, and shall file it, with the summary Technical Manual Sec. B-17.2 requires, with the annual compliance report. The County publishes the log with that report.

(e) Complaints to the County. The operator shall forward to the County's code enforcement official, within [__] business days, any complaint alleging a violation of this Chapter. It shall include the complainant's name and address only if the complainant consents after being told that a complaint forwarded to the County is a public record. Under ss. 125.69(4)(b), 162.06(1)(b), and 162.21(3)(b), Florida Statutes, a code inspector may not initiate enforcement proceedings, and a code enforcement officer or a code inspector designated under s. 125.69(4) may not initiate an investigation, by way of an anonymous complaint, unless the inspector or officer has reason to believe that the violation presents an imminent threat to public health, safety, or welfare or imminent destruction of habitat or sensitive resources. Nothing in this Section limits any person's right to complain directly to the County, or any enforcement under Sec. 18.

(f) No retaliation. The operator shall not retaliate against any person for making a complaint.

Sec. 18. Enforcement.

(a) Inspection. The County may inspect on reasonable notice, consistent with facility security protocols, to verify compliance with this Chapter. Where the County reasonably suspects an unsafe condition, the operator shall provide access within [__] hours after notice. If access is not given, the County may apply for an inspection warrant under ss. 933.20 to 933.30, Florida Statutes. Refusal of an entry that no warrant authorizes is not a violation of this Chapter, and nothing in this Section limits entry in an emergency.

(b) Penalties and injunction. Violations are punishable as provided by s. 125.69, Florida Statutes, and enforceable under chapter 162, Florida Statutes. Each day of continuing violation is a separate violation. The County may seek injunctive relief, the standards of this Chapter protecting interests for which legal remedies are inadequate.

(c) Curtailment. Upon a violation of Sec. 7, Sec. 8, or Sec. 12(a), (c), or (d) not cured within the period this Chapter allows, the County may require curtailment of the offending equipment or system, other than a withdrawal of water, until compliance is restored, in the manner Sec. 9(d) provides for sound. No curtailment shall be required where the fire official determines in writing that it would create a greater hazard to life or property; the operator shall then implement the measure the fire official approves, which shall achieve compliance as far as safety allows.

(d) Development orders. Upon a violation not cured within the applicable period, the County may withhold or suspend further development orders, certificates of occupancy, and building permits as to land within the Development Unit until compliance is restored, except a building permit whose plans comply with the codes that s. 553.79(2)(a)4., Florida Statutes, names, and a development order to the extent it issues such a permit. A development permit or development order applied for, as s. 125.022(3), Florida Statutes, defines those terms, is withheld only by a written denial within the periods that section allows. The denial states its findings and cites this subsection, and it is without prejudice to a new application once compliance is restored. This subsection does not authorize revocation of a certificate of occupancy in effect for occupied space, does not apply to permits sought to cure the violation, and does not extend or suspend any period stated in Sec. 15A(f) as to any application by a person not in violation.

(e) Irreparable harm. The Board finds that violations of Secs. 7, 8, 9, 10, 11A, and 12(a), (c), and (d) may result in irreparable or irreversible harm within the meaning of s. 162.09(2)(a) and (d), Florida Statutes, and directs the County's code enforcement board or special magistrate to consider that finding in fixing any fine.

Sec. 19. Existing Facilities.

(a) Nonconforming status. A Covered Facility lawfully established before the effective date of this Chapter, and a Tier 2 data center lawfully established before the effective date of Sec. 15A, is a lawful nonconforming use governed by the County's generally applicable nonconforming use provisions, except as Sec. 1(b), this Section, and Sec. 15A(g) provide.

(b) Repair and replacement. An existing facility may continue and may be repaired, maintained, and re-equipped. When a system is replaced, the replacement shall comply with the standards of Secs. 7 through 14, other than Secs. 11(a), 11(b), and 12(b), that apply to that system, and Sec. 5(h) measures a replacement BESS as an improvement first shown on the site plan or permit that authorizes its replacement.

(c) Voluntary compliance. The owner or operator of a nonconforming Covered Facility may apply for a determination that the use conforms to this Chapter. The application shall include a compliance schedule identifying each standard of Secs. 5 through 14, the means of compliance, and the date by which compliance will be achieved. For the determination, an improvement that a site plan approved before the effective date of this Chapter first showed is measured under Sec. 5(h) as the property lines, properties, rights-of-way, residential structures, and Sensitive Receptors exist on that effective date.

(d) Alternative compliance. Where a standard cannot be met because of the existing configuration of structures lawfully constructed before the effective date, the applicant may propose alternative compliance. The County Administrator or designee may approve it only on a written finding, supported by a certification of a Florida-licensed professional engineer or other qualified professional, that the proposed measures achieve substantially the protective effect of the standard or, where they do not, that no feasible measure would and that the residual impact is mitigated to the extent practicable. The approval shall state the standard, the prescribed method, the alternative approved, and the basis for the finding, and shall be included in the record of the determination. Alternative compliance is not available as to Sec. 5(d) to (g), Sec. 7(a)(1), Sec. 11(a), Sec. 11A, or Sec. 14.

(e) Effect of determination. Upon the County's written determination that the compliance schedule has been performed, the use is a conforming use for all purposes of the County's land development regulations. The determination is a ministerial confirmation of performance and is not a variance, a rezoning, a development order, or a special exception.

(f) Revocation. A determination under subsection (e) is subject to revocation, after notice and an opportunity to cure, upon a finding that the use has ceased to comply with a standard on which the determination rested.

(g) No status created for exchange. Nothing in this Chapter shall be construed to create or continue nonconforming status for the purpose of inducing any applicant to enter into any agreement or make any contribution. Relief under this Section is available on compliance alone.
