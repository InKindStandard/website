FLORIDA MODEL INSTRUMENTS FOR DATA CENTER DEVELOPMENT

COUNTY PROCEDURE AND RECORDS KIT

Staff report, resolution, and adoption records · Model forms for Florida counties

Model form. Remove this heading block, and every drafting note, before the County first makes the draft public.

What this is. The kit holds the County's working forms: a staff report for each Tier 3 application (Part A), an insert for Sec. 15A reviews (Part B), a resolution of decision (Part C), the business impact estimate (Part D), the hearing calendar (Part E), a gap check against existing rules (Part F), and a model plan policy on data center water sources (Part G). Staff complete it for each application or ordinance; it adopts nothing by itself.

What it does. The table after this heading block says when each Part is used.

Works with. The forms record the County's review under Exhibit A (Data Center Standards), Exhibit B (Technical Manual) and, where adopted, Sec. 15A (Module M1), which makes Tier 2 and Tier 3 data centers conditional uses decided on stated findings within set review periods. A development agreement is a separate item after the decision (Part A, Section 13), and its impact findings, Exhibit "B-1," are not Exhibit B.

Before use. Where the kit repeats a bracketed figure of the Standards, use the figure the County adopted. Keep Part B only where Sec. 15A is adopted, the site plan paragraph of Part A, Section 3, only where Sec. 15(g) designates later site plans for administrative review, and the bracketed alternative in Part A, Section 13, only where no deposit under Sec. 15(f) paid for Exhibit "B-1." Give no notice of an ordinance or the plan policy before the County Attorney's written determination (Part E; Table G-2).

Known gaps. No county has adopted this model, and no licensed attorney has reviewed it. No impact fee Technical Study or Exhibit "B-1" has been prepared on these models. Some forms rest on law that is not settled: the County's part at a water management district hearing (Part A, Section 6); a denial under Sec. 18(d) (Part A, Section 10, and Part C); whether an emergency declaration counts under s. 252.422 (Part E); and the reach of s. 373.262(2) and the optional sentence on evaporative cooling (Part G). The Open Legal Questions give the current reading of the last three.

13. Separate item: development agreement

Complete only where a development agreement under ss. 163.3220 to 163.3243, Florida Statutes, is before the Board. The agreement is a separate agenda item, separately noticed under s. 163.3225 and separately voted. It is taken up after the decision on the application, is not a criterion for approval, and is not weighed in Sections 5, 7, 10, or 11 (Sec. 16(b)). Once it is executed, an uncured default in an obligation that mitigates an impact may support a written denial of a later application, and only as Sec. 16(e) provides.

The agreement carries no item given in exchange for an approval. Besides mitigation of impacts that its impact study (Exhibit "B-1") measures, it carries only statutory concurrency terms (Agreement Section 2.4) and County purchases at cost (Agreement Section 4.5) (In-Kind Eligibility and Characterization Test, Part 5 and Sec. 5.1). PART G. PLAN POLICY ON DATA CENTER WATER SOURCES

Use this Part where the County will state the water source rules of Sec. 7(a)(1) and (2) in its comprehensive plan as well as in its land development regulations. Since 1 July 2026 a water management district "may not issue a permit to a large-scale data center applicant for an allocation of water if the proposed use of the water is harmful to the water resources of the area or is prohibited by the applicable local government zoning regulations and comprehensive plan" (s. 373.262(2), Florida Statutes, created by Chapter 2026-65, Laws of Florida). The policy brings within that subsection only a large-scale data center's own application to withdraw water (s. 373.203(4), Florida Statutes). It does not reach potable water a public water system supplies, or groundwater a third party withdraws under its own permit. Whether the prohibition must appear in both the code and the plan, and how far the subsection reaches, are open (Open Legal Questions, Q52; Counsel 52.2).
