For sponsors and funders

The project, for a sponsor's or funder's review

What a fiscal sponsor or a funder would want to check, in one place: what exists, who decides, how the work is paid for, what it plans and what it does not yet have.

This page asks no one for money, property or anything else of value. The project is not asking the public for gifts yet. Once there is a fiscal sponsor, gifts of money would go through it, never in anyone's own name.

2 · Who decides

One person today, under written rules

The founder decides the content and what is published, and steps aside from any work a tie touches. Six policies, a code of conduct and an advisory committee charter set the rules; the founder's record of ties is published.

Forming: an advisory committee of three to five people without a stake, to decide complaints about the founder. Seeking: a fiscal sponsor.

Governance and policies

3 · How the work is paid for

By the founder, so far

No one but the founder has paid for it: the founder pays its small costs himself, and the project has received no money or other gifts. Funders so far: none. Fees so far: none.

Every gift or grant from an organization will be published with the funder's name, the amount and its purpose, and no gift is accepted from an organization that will not be named; individuals who give $5,000 or more in a year will be published by name and amount, and smaller gifts as a total.

5 · Risks, and how the project handles them

What a careful reviewer would ask

Each answer is a rule the project has written down, or a gap it states.

QuestionHow the project handles it
LobbyingThe project does not lobby, with one exception: a statement on a ballot measure that its neutrality policy allows, which is counted as lobbying. No one acting for it urges a legislator, a commission or any other body to vote a particular way on specific legislation or an ordinance. Staff briefings that explain options without urging a vote are education, not lobbying; the policy has counsel confirm that line, and no lawyer has yet.
Legal relianceNothing here is legal advice. Every document states its known gaps. A lawyer's review of the model documents is planned once its money is in hand.
Taking sidesThe neutrality policy requires the six checks of the Neutrality Test before an item goes out. The test is new and so far tried on only a few items; a pass shows that its checks were made, not that the result is fair.
Money from interested partiesNone, at any time, from developers, owners, operators or sponsors of the kinds of projects its published model documents cover, or from a utility that is or may be a party to an agreement built on them, or from a company tied to them. None from anyone else with a direct financial stake in how those documents are written or applied. The Gift Acceptance Policy sets the details.
AI toolsThey help draft and check the work, and the founder reviews every item and decides what is published, except work the founder steps aside from because of a tie. No AI tool decides the content of the model documents.
One personToday the project is the work of one person, the founder. An advisory committee is not yet formed; the founder is looking for its members.
PrivacyThis site collects nothing of its own about its visitors: no cookies, no analytics and no forms. What its host logs is set out in the privacy statement.

6 · What the project does not yet have

  • No county has adopted the model documents, and no lawyer has reviewed them.
  • The project has no organization of its own yet, and no fiscal sponsor (an established nonprofit that would oversee the work and hold its money) or board.
  • Complaints about the founder's own work wait, with no date set, for an advisory committee not yet formed.
  • The project's policies are drafts the founder has approved; no organization has adopted them, and no lawyer has reviewed them.
