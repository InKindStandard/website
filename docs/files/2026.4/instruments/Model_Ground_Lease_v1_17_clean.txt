**Section 15.10 Force Majeure.**

(a) **Defined.** “Force majeure” means an event beyond the reasonable control of the Party claiming it, not caused by its fault, negligence, or breach of this Lease, that prevents its performance despite its reasonable efforts, and is limited to: a hurricane, tropical storm, tornado, flood, wildfire, earthquake, or sinkhole; a fire or explosion; war, terrorism, sabotage, riot, or civil disorder; an epidemic or an order of a public health authority; a failure of the regional electric transmission system or of a public water or wastewater system; a strike or other labor disturbance that is general in the region or in an industry and is not directed only at that Party or its contractors; and an order of a court or of a governmental authority, other than the County acting as landlord under this Lease. Force majeure does not include (1) economic or market conditions, an increase in cost, or the unavailability of financing; (2) a delay or shortage in the supply of equipment, materials, or labor, or a delay of a contractor or supplier, unless itself caused by an event this subsection names; (3) a delay in utility service, interconnection, or a permit or approval; (4) the financial condition of the Party claiming it; or (5) an event that the obligation claimed to be excused exists to meet, except as Section 14.5 provides.

(b) **Notice; mitigation.** The Party claiming force majeure shall give the other Party written notice within [__] days after it learns of the event, stating the event, the obligations affected, and the expected period of prevention; shall use reasonable efforts to mitigate the event’s effect and to resume performance; and shall give written notice when performance resumes. An event noticed late excuses performance only from the date of notice.

(c) **Effect.** Force majeure extends the time for performing a non-monetary obligation, other than a Health and Safety Standard or an Emergency Obligation, by the period performance is prevented and a restoration period after the event of not more than [__] days, and does not otherwise excuse performance. It never excuses rent, any other monetary obligation, or the maintenance of any security. A Health and Safety Standard is excused only while, and to the extent that, performing it is physically impossible, with no restoration period, and Section 14.5 governs the Emergency Obligations.

Additional information regarding radon and radon testing may be obtained from your county health department. (Section 404.056(5), Florida Statutes.)
