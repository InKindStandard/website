Plain Language Guide

What the model documents do, sector by sector

The model documents cover three kinds of large project in Florida. The set for data centers is published, in Release 2026.4. The sets for logistics facilities and automated manufacturing are drafts: they are not published yet, and they may change before they are.

This page sums up each set. Where it and a document differ, the document governs. Not legal advice.

Every sector

What is the same in every set

Four places, each with its own rules

Standards. Rules every project must meet, in the county's own code. A standard is never traded for anything.

An impact fee or a development agreement. Payments and measures that answer an impact the county's own study measures.

A lease of county land. Here the county acts as a landowner. Cash rent comes first, and the Board may take some rent in kind on top of it.

A private community agreement. Benefits that are not mitigation, agreed between the developer and an existing community organization. The county is not a party.

The rules that hold it together

Every county payment rests on a study. Each amount stays blank until a study supplies a range, and the Board picks a figure within it. Florida law puts the burden on the county to show that a condition fits the impact.

The county stays out of private agreements. Its staff do not refer groups to developers, join the talks or comment on drafts, and no decision may take such an agreement into account.

New rules may have to wait. After the 2024 hurricanes, state law bars every Florida county and city from adopting more restrictive land development rules until late 2027. The county attorney decides in writing whether a new rule is caught.

Two tests sort the ideas. The first decides whether an item belongs in any document, and which one. The second applies only where a bargain is lawful: a lease of county land, and the community organization's own use of a private agreement.

Sector by sector

Three sets, in the order a county would use them

Published

Data centers

Release 2026.4, in eight steps: whether new rules may be adopted yet; the Data Center Standards; the forms staff use; the studies; an impact fee for each category a study supports; a development agreement, for mitigation only; emergency power at county facilities, if the county chooses; and a lease, if the county leases its own land.

Only this set has a model impact fee ordinance. In the private agreement, the community organization chooses whether the developer's computing share comes as money, the default, as computing time for public use, or as both.

Still to come: no county has commissioned the studies, so every fee and amount is blank.

The full guide for data centers

Draft

Logistics facilities

Large warehouses, distribution and delivery centers, cold storage, truck terminals and hazardous materials warehouses, in eight steps. Standards place a facility in one of three tiers by floor area, and set its distance from homes, schools and parks, its lighting and sound, its truck access, and restroom access for drivers.

There is no model impact fee ordinance. A county may consider a housing linkage fee, and an incentive agreement attaches job and wage conditions to a tax exemption or grant the Board has already chosen to give. Cities have their own editions.

Still to come: the floor-area figures are placeholders, and a few terms, such as truck routes, wait on a lawyer's advice.

Coming soon. Not yet published.

Draft

Automated manufacturing

Any large plant that makes or assembles goods, in six steps. This set adds no standards: the county's own code is the rule every plant meets. The study weighs eighteen kinds of impact, three of them only for a plant on the water.

On county land, the lease may take rent in kind for emergency services only, such as a staging lot or boat berths. If the Board grants an incentive, the business gives notice before automation ends jobs, and offers those workers new roles and paid retraining, with severance for any not placed.

Still to come: a few terms wait on a lawyer's advice, and no rule yet keeps the county out of a private agreement for a plant that signs no development agreement.

Coming soon. Not yet published.

Logistics and manufacturing

What Florida law lets a county require of an employer

A county or city may not set heat rules beyond state and federal law, or regulate work schedules. It may set wages or benefits only in narrow cases, chiefly as a condition of a tax break or subsidy. So in the drafts, only the incentive agreement carries wage or benefit terms, and a private agreement binds only the developer and the organization that signs it.

Every sector

What no set can do

  • Stand in for a lawyer. No lawyer has reviewed the documents, and no county has adopted them.
  • Make a payment lawful without a study, or trade a standard for anything.
  • Require a private agreement, or give the county a role in one.
  • Change a tax or set a utility rate.

The terms used here are explained in the glossary of the guide for data centers.
