Governance

Who decides, and the rules the project keeps

Today one person decides, under written rules. This page says what is in place, what is forming, and what changes as the project grows.

Who decides

Today, and as the project grows

Today

The founder

Bound by the project's policies. AI tools help draft and check the work, and the founder reviews every item and decides what is published, except work the founder steps aside from because of a tie.

Forming

An advisory committee

Three to five people without a stake, unpaid. It advises the founder, decides complaints about the founder, and receives the founder's own disclosures of ties.

Seeking

A fiscal sponsor

An established nonprofit that would oversee the work and hold its money. Where its gift rules and the project's differ, the project asks that the stricter apply.

Later

A Standards Council

To decide the content of the model documents.

The policies

The rules the project holds itself to

Drafts the founder has approved. No organization has adopted them, and no lawyer has reviewed them. Each states its known gaps first.

The advisory committee

Three to five people, without a stake

Members serve unpaid, in a personal capacity, and none may have a work, money or property tie to a covered project or to anyone who develops, owns or operates one. Balance by region, sector and county type is an aim, not a test.

Coming soon: how to take part.

Complaints and corrections

About the project's own work

Write to contact@inkindstandard.org. Reports of factual errors that show the official record or text are checked and, if wrong, corrected now. Because the founder prepared and approved all the published work, every complaint about it waits, with no date set, for an advisory committee not yet formed; the founder is looking for its members.

Complaints about a company or a county go to the public body concerned, such as a county's code enforcement office. The project does not take them.
