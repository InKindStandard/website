Our aim

Basics that cost less, and stay that way

Much of what a household spends goes to a few basics: a home, food, water and power. We are working toward basics that cost less and stay that way, built one practical step at a time, each one something you can check.

In practice, that could mean free plans for a small home or a hurricane-rated greenhouse, or a kit for opening a repair shop.

This aim sets no rule for anyone. A county can use the model documents, or none of them, without sharing it.

It has happened before

Big change can come in practical steps

In the mid-1930s, nine out of ten rural American homes had no electricity. By 1953, more than 90 percent of farms had it, much of it through cooperatives that rural people owned themselves and ran not for profit. No single breakthrough did that. Federal loans helped pay for the lines, and local cooperatives built them, one area at a time.

Figures from the National Rural Electric Cooperative Association.

The steps

Where each part stands

Only the model documents, records and guides are published today, and no county has used the documents yet. Everything else starts only once its conditions are met. The programs are separate, and do not depend on any county using the documents.

Today

1. Free documents, records and guides

A county and its residents can see what a large project could bring and cost them, and decide knowing the facts, including using none of the documents.

Published, if a county chooses

2. A project that pays for what it adds

Each fee or mitigation payment rests on a study of the costs the project adds. A county can also pay for spare conduit in the developer's trenches, and, if it leases its own land, take computing time for education on top of full cash rent.

Planned

3. More places, more kinds of projects

Logistics facilities, automated manufacturing, Georgia and South Carolina, once each one's law is mapped and the people and money are in place.

Planned

4. Open designs and kits

Free, openly licensed designs, such as small homes and hurricane-rated greenhouses, and step-by-step kits for starting local businesses, such as repair shops.

Planned

5. Checked, and dropped if it fails

Any program would be dropped if an independent evaluation found that it does not lower costs.

What comes next, and what each plan needs first

What could change for a household

Some basics are easier to reach than others

The model documents touch some basics far more than others. This table is our own judgment of how the documents work. It is not a measurement, and no study has tested it.

How directlyWhat it touchesWhy
Most directlyLearning and internet accessComputing time for public use, and spare conduit for future internet lines. No county has used these terms yet.
A littlePower and emergency servicesWhere a study finds need and benefit, a project pays for the extra emergency facilities it calls for, including backup power. That covers a cost it adds, and lowers no household's bills.
Hardly at allHousing and foodThese depend on land, wages and prices that one project barely touches. The planned open designs aim at them.

Limits

What stays scarce, and what we don't do

Not everything can get cheaper. Land in a given place, water where it is short, people's time, and things valued because they are rare will stay scarce, and we do not promise otherwise.

We do not tell any county whether to approve a project, and we take no side between parties or candidates. We set no taxes and fund no county services. In the model documents, a standard is never traded for anything. And we set no dates for things that are not yet real.

Checking our work

How you'll know if it's working

Today, no county has adopted the model documents, and no lawyer has reviewed them. No program has started, so there are no results to show yet. Every county action in the public records is checked against the official record, and corrections are published on Updates and corrections.

What we offer

A direction, not a forecast

This is a direction, not a forecast or a promise, because some things will always be scarce. What we offer is the next step, done in the open, and a way to check it.
