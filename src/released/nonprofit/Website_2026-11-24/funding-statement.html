Who prepares this work, and who pays for it

This page is about In-Kind Standard, a citizen-prepared, nonpartisan project that writes free model documents Florida counties may use when a large data center seeks to locate there, and keeps a public record of what Florida counties and cities have done. The work is prepared by one person: its founder, a private citizen. AI tools help draft and check the work, and the founder reviews every item and decides what is published, except work the founder steps aside from because of a tie, meaning a connection that might be, or look like, a conflict of interest. Such work waits until someone without the tie can prepare and check it (Conflict of Interest Policy, section 3.5). The founder's ties, and the decisions on them, are listed in "The Founder's Ties and Decisions" (the same policy, sections 3.4 and 9). Once the project's main website is live, it will show that list on the page that says who prepares the work; until then, and afterwards too, the list is sent to anyone who asks at contact@inkindstandard.org.

No client, funder, company, county, government or political party asks for the work, directs it or reviews it before it is published. No one but the founder has paid for it: the founder pays its small costs himself, and the project has received no money or other gifts. Every gift or grant from an organization will be published with the funder's name, the amount and its purpose, and no gift is accepted from an organization that will not be named; individuals who give $5,000 or more in a year will be published by name and amount, and smaller gifts as a total (Gift Acceptance Policy, section 5).

The project takes no money or other gifts, at any time, from developers, owners, operators or sponsors of data centers, of any size and in any state, or of a facility of another kind that its published model documents cover, in a state for which they are published. Nor does it take any, at any time, from a utility that is or may be a party to an agreement built on its model documents, or from a company it has found tied to such data centers or facilities (its funder screen); and it takes none from anyone else with a direct financial stake in how those documents are written or applied, while the stake lasts and, after it ends, for a waiting period still to be set (until then, such a gift is decided in the open). It takes none from political parties, campaigns, political committees or organizations whose main purpose is partisan, from any foreign government, or from a "foreign source of concern" as Florida law defines it (among them the governments, officials, political parties and organizations of the countries the law names, such as China and Russia, and people living there who are not U.S. citizens; the Gift Acceptance Policy, section 2.5, lists them all).

Gifts from advocacy groups, unions and business associations (other than trade associations of those with a direct financial stake, which are barred) are decided in the open, and are usually declined if the group campaigns for or against data centers or the other facilities its published model documents cover, on either side (the Gift Acceptance Policy, sections 2.2, 2.7 and 2.8).

The project explains, and never urges a county to decide a particular way (Political Neutrality Policy). The project has no organization of its own yet, and no fiscal sponsor (an established nonprofit that would oversee the work and hold its money) or board. No county has adopted its model documents, and no lawyer has reviewed the work.

Funders so far: none.

Fees so far: none. Fees for the project's courses and other services, such as talks, are published here each year as a total, apart from gifts, with the part paid by public bodies (Gift Acceptance Policy, section 5.4).
