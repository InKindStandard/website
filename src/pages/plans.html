What comes next, and what each plan needs first

More kinds of projects and more states come first. Beyond the model documents, the project plans programs to lower the cost of essentials such as food, housing, water and energy. Each starts only once its conditions are met, and is dropped if an independent evaluation finds that it does not lower costs.

No program includes any part of the model documents, and no gift for a program reaches them.

Keeping the published work sound

Planned

A lawyer's review of the model documents

Land use counsel reviews the main open legal questions the model documents raise.

Starts when: its money is in hand.

Planned

A yearly review and a public report

State law and the model documents reviewed once a year, with a public report on neutrality, gifts and complaints.

When: once a year, with an outside review once there is a board.

Planned

One live online course of about a day

On the model documents, at one published price, set before the first run, for county staff and officials and anyone else without a direct financial stake in how the documents are written or applied, with a free recording of each run published for everyone. It explains every option, including adopting none of the documents, and gives no advice on any county's own application.

When: not yet scheduled.

Programs to lower the cost of essentials

Open designs, kits, contests and commissions

Planned

Open designs and kits

Free, openly licensed designs, such as small homes and hurricane-rated greenhouses, and kits (step-by-step guides with templates) for starting local businesses, such as repair shops.

Starts when: at first, among other conditions, a gift for that purpose and a fiscal sponsor that agrees to run it.

Planned

Contests and paid commissions

To make them, each design reviewed independently before it enters the library and, as a rule, designed so that its core use keeps working without its automation.

Starts when: as for the designs.

An open design library
